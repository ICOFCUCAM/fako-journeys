Sudan

More pyramids than Egypt.

Two hundred Nubian pyramids at Meroe and Nuri, the Nile making its great bend through desert, and a Red Sea coast with reefs nobody dives — a country with an extraordinary past and a hard present.

Wildlife

Granite hills behind Port Sudan, and a volcanic crater lake on Jebel Marra in Darfur.

Landscapes
  • The Great BendThe Nile turns southwest for three hundred kilometres here, against every expectation.
  • The Red Sea Hills and Jebel MarraGranite hills behind Port Sudan, and a volcanic crater lake on Jebel Marra in Darfur.
  • The CataractsSix of them on the Nile, rapids where the river crosses granite, and boats have to portage.
  • The Blue and the WhiteThey meet at Khartoum, one clear and one brown, and you can see the line between them.
  • Reefs Nobody DivesSanganeb atoll and Sha'ab Rumi, where Cousteau sank Conshelf II and the coral grew over it.
  • Acacia and Doum PalmGum arabic comes from the acacia belt across the middle of the country. Most of the world's does.
Wildlife
  • Dinder and the Red SeaDinder on the Ethiopian border holds lion, giraffe and roan; the reefs hold everything else.
  • DinderOne of Africa's oldest national parks, and one of the least visited on the continent.
  • Gum Arabic and the Acacia BeltA gum tapped from wild acacia by hand, in most of the soft drinks in the world.
  • Walking Between PyramidsThere is no fence at Meroe. You walk in off the sand and up the ridge.
People and culture
  • Nubian, Arab, BejaNubian along the northern Nile, Beja in the Red Sea hills, and nearly six hundred groups altogether.
  • The NubiansThe Nubian villages along the northern Nile paint their houses, and have done for a very long time.
  • Sufi Friday at OmdurmanThe Hamed al-Nil tomb, every Friday at sunset, drumming and whirling for two hours.
  • Basketry and SilverCoiled palm baskets from the north, Beja swords, and silver from the Omdurman souk.
  • Ful and KisraFul medames for breakfast everywhere, kisra sorghum flatbread, and coffee spiced with ginger.
  • The Nile at TutiThe island in the confluence, farmed, walkable, and reached by ferry from three sides.
  • The Camel MarketOmdurman's camel market on a Saturday, with animals in from Kordofan and Darfur.
Places
  • Khartoum and OmdurmanTwo cities facing each other across the confluence, one colonial grid and one enormous souk.
  • Mud Brick and Painted DoorsNubian vaulted mud-brick houses with painted gates, a technique revived by Hassan Fathy.
  • Meroe, Nuri and El-KurruThree royal cemeteries of the Kingdom of Kush, which ruled Egypt for a century.
  • KushGebel Barkal was the holy mountain of two kingdoms, and the temple of Amun is cut into it.
Ways to travel
  • Diving and Desert DrivingLiveaboards out of Port Sudan, and four-wheel-drive routes between the pyramid fields.
  • Very LittleA handful of Red Sea liveaboards, and camps at Meroe. That is the whole of it.
  • Pyramids and DunesMeroe an hour after dawn, when the sand is still ridged and the shadows are long.
  • Old DongolaA Christian Nubian capital on the Nile, with a throne hall that became a mosque.
