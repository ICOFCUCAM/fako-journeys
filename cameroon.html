01The six

Six climates. One Kamerun.

You can cross five ecological zones in this country without leaving it. These are the six our circuits are built along, in the order you would drive them — height above sea level, and what stands on it.

0 m Coast

Black sand at Limbe, the Lobé going over rock straight into the Atlantic, pirogues out at first light.

4,095 m Volcano

An active volcano straight out of the sea. Rainforest, then grass, then lava field, then nothing but cinder.

150 m Rainforest

Korup: one of the oldest forests in Africa. Canopy bridge over the Mana, drill monkeys, 400 tree species.

1,800 m Highlands

The Ring Road, the crater lakes, tea at Ndawara, and villages that have stood a very long time.

1,200 m Kingdoms & culture

Foumban, the Bamoun palace and a craft street where the casting and weaving are still work, not display.

320 m Savanna

The Bénoué and then Waza. Flat acacia country, elephant and giraffe drawn onto the waterholes from March.

Douala 4°03'N · Waza 11°23'N Road transfer Douala–Maroua: 2 days, or 1h45 by air Every circuit runs bilingual, French and English
Day two · Hut 2 to the summit cone

Above three thousand the forest simply stops.

You leave Buea in wet green at a thousand metres and by the second afternoon there is nothing growing at all — black cinder, old lava, and the sea a very long way underneath. Guides carry the water, porters carry the packs, and the pace is set by the slowest walker on purpose.

02b — The highland kingdoms

Foumban is not a museum stop. It is a working craft town.

The Bamoun dynasty has kept its records since 1394 and the palace at Foumban still houses the court. Behind it runs the Rue des Artisans, where the bronze casters, the embroiderers and the wood carvers work in the open and will let you stand in the doorway for as long as you like. We time the visit for a weekday morning, when the foundries are lit.

Kribi · the last hour of light

The only waterfall on this coast that never reaches a river. It goes straight into the sea.

Nobody photographs the Lobé well, and nobody minds. You hear it before the track opens out, you stand on warm black sand while it happens, and then somebody sells you grilled fish and you sit down.

05When to come

Two rainy seasons, and they are not the same rain.

The south-west coast is among the wettest places on earth — Debundscha takes about ten metres of rain a year. The Far North is Sahel and takes barely one. Which half of the country you want decides your month, so book the country to the calendar rather than the other way round.

Nov – FebDry, everywhere

The one window where the whole country works at once. Best trekking, best northern game viewing, harmattan haze in the north from December.

Mar – MayNorth at its best

Hot — 38°C at Waza — and that is the point: the waterholes concentrate everything. Coast begins to break into storms by late April.

Jun – SepBuea rains

The mountain closes for good reason: the summit route becomes a river of scree. Highlands and Foumban stay entirely workable, and everything is green.

OctThe turn

Rain easing, highlands at their greenest, prices lower and nobody about. Our own favourite month, with the caveat that roads in the north-west stay soft.

Is February really the busiest month?

On the mountain, yes — the Race of Hope fills Buea in the third week and hut space goes early. We hold beds from October for February departures.

Can one trip cover north and south?

Comfortably in fourteen days if you fly one internal leg. In ten days you will spend three of them on the road, which we would rather you did not.
