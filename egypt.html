Landscapes
  • The Nile and the DesertA green ribbon a few kilometres wide, and sand for a thousand kilometres either side of it.
  • SinaiMount Sinai and Saint Catherine's, and granite peaks over two thousand metres above the Red Sea.
  • The First CataractThe Nile's cataracts at Aswan — rapids rather than falls, and the reason the boats stopped here.
  • The Nile ItselfFeluccas under sail at Aswan, and a river that has been the country's main road for millennia.
  • The Red SeaSome of the clearest reef diving on earth, an hour's flight from the pyramids.
  • The FayoumNot forest — the Fayoum's palm and olive, and the acacia of Wadi el-Rayan.
Wildlife
  • Birds of the DeltaTwo million migrating birds use the Delta and the Red Sea coast as a flyway each year.
  • The Western DesertNot big game — desert fox, gazelle and the silence of the Great Sand Sea.
  • Reef and OasisReef protection on the Red Sea, and oasis agriculture that has managed water for millennia.
  • Dive, Sail, RideReef diving at Marsa Alam, feluccas at Aswan, and camels in the Western Desert.
People and culture
  • Coptic, Islamic, PharaonicThree layers still in daily use, sometimes in the same street in old Cairo.
  • Nubians and BedouinNubian villages above Aswan and Bedouin communities in the Sinai and the Western Desert.
  • Moulids and RamadanThe moulid processions, and Cairo at iftar when the whole city eats at once.
  • Papyrus, Glass and CottonHand-blown glass in Cairo, Egyptian cotton, and papyrus made the way it always was.
  • Ful, Koshary and BreadBreakfast is ful and bread; lunch is koshary; both are older than most cuisines.
  • Straightforward with ChildrenShort internal flights, boats instead of buses, and a story at every stop.
  • The Corniche at DuskFamilies out along the river, tea on the pavement, and the traffic finally slowing.
Places
  • Cairo and AlexandriaTwenty million people on the Nile, and a Mediterranean city that remembers being Greek.
  • Mamluk CairoThe mosques, sabils and madrasas of the medieval city, largely still standing.
  • The Valley of the KingsSixty-odd tombs cut into a single dry valley, most of them painted floor to ceiling.
  • Karnak, Luxor, Abu SimbelKarnak took two thousand years to build; Abu Simbel was moved sixty metres to save it.
Ways to travel
  • Desert CrossingsDiving the Red Sea, walking the Sinai with Bedouin guides, and crossing the sand sea by vehicle.
  • Dahabiyas and Desert CampsSmall sailing dahabiyas instead of the big cruisers, and camps in the White Desert.
  • The Light at SixDesert light at either end of the day is the whole reason to be up early here.
  • SiwaEight hundred kilometres west, a Berber-speaking oasis with its own language and salt lakes.
