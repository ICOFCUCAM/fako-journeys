West Africa Nigeria

One in five Africans is Nigerian. Afrobeats, Nollywood, the Benin bronzes, Yankari's elephants and the Obudu plateau — a country that exports its culture faster than anywhere else on earth.

Cape Town is the easy answer and a beautiful one. Lagos is twenty million people making most of the music, film and fashion the rest of the continent is dancing to, and almost nobody puts it first.

Explore Nigeria →

Start where the forest is still breathing.

East Africa Uganda

Half the world's mountain gorillas, the source of the Nile, the Rwenzori snowline on the equator and savanna parks within a day's drive of each other.

Not the Serengeti first. Half the world's mountain gorillas live on three volcanoes, and you walk to them.

Explore Uganda →

Begin where the coast remembers.

West Africa Ghana

The most straightforward country in West Africa to travel, with the Atlantic slave forts of the coast, the Ashanti kingdom at Kumasi, Mole's elephants in the north, and a music scene worth planning around.

Cape Coast and Elmina are kept as they were, the Ashanti court still sits at Kumasi, and there is a band on every night.

Explore Ghana →

Skip the resorts on the other coast.

Southern Africa Mozambique

The Indian Ocean coast of southern Africa — the Bazaruto and Quirimbas archipelagos, Ilha de Moçambique, Gorongosa's restored wildlife, and a Portuguese-Swahili culture that exists nowhere else.

Two thousand kilometres of Indian Ocean, most of it still without a road, and dhows instead of speedboats.

Explore Mozambique →

Go west of the famous forests.

Central & West Africa Cameroon

Six ecological zones in nine hundred kilometres: an active 4,095 m volcano, Atlantic black sand, Korup rainforest, the Grassfields kingdoms and the Sahel edge at Waza.

Korup is older than the Congo basin's and has a canopy walkway thirty metres up, with almost nobody on it — and the same country runs from that forest to an active volcano and out to the Sahel.

Explore Cameroon →

Eat where the bread is still communal.

North Africa Morocco

Four thousand metres of High Atlas within three hours of the Atlantic, four imperial cities, and the edge of the Sahara at Merzouga — a country you can cross from surf to dunes in two days.

Tagine cooked slowly, bread baked in the neighbourhood oven, and a souk that sells to the people who live there.

Explore Morocco →

The oldest journey there is.

North Africa Egypt

The oldest continuous tourism in the world, and still the reason most people first think of Africa — the Giza plateau, the Valley of the Kings, Abu Simbel, and the Red Sea reefs on the other coast.

People have travelled to see this for two and a half thousand years, and the Valley of the Kings is still painted floor to ceiling.

Explore Egypt →

Take the road with nobody on it.

Southern Africa Namibia

The oldest desert on earth running into the Atlantic — Sossusvlei's dunes, the Skeleton Coast, Etosha's waterholes and Damaraland's desert elephant, across a country with almost nobody in it.

The best self-drive country on the continent: gravel, dunes, and the darkest measured night sky anywhere.

Explore Namibia →

04The scale

Africa is bigger than the map in your head.

Ouidah is the source of vodun and a departure point of the Atlantic slave trade, Abomey holds the earthen palaces of the kings of Dahomey, and Ganvie is a town of thirty thousand people on stilts in a lagoon.

November to February, the main dry season. The Vodun festival is on 10 January.

Walk out of it into Togo, Niger, Burkina Faso and Nigeria.

Four-fifths Sahara, a Mediterranean coast of Roman cities, and the Hoggar and Tassili massifs in the deep south — rock art nine thousand years old on a plateau the size of Britain.

Walk out of it into Algeria, Sudan, Egypt, Niger, Tunisia and Chad.

Cabo Verde

Ten Atlantic islands six hundred kilometres off Senegal: Fogo is an active volcano with vineyards inside the caldera, Santo Antao is cut into ribeiras you walk down, and the morna that Cesaria Evora sang is on the UNESCO list.

November to May. The wind for kitesurfing on Sal and Boa Vista is strongest from December to March.

Explore Cabo Verde →

Comoros

Karthala is one of the most active volcanoes in the world with a crater four kilometres across, the coelacanth was found alive off these islands after eighty million years, and Moroni's stone town is the least visited Swahili port on the coast.

May to October, the dry season. Cyclones are possible from December to March.

Explore Comoros →

Madagascar

Separated from Africa 160 million years ago, with ninety per cent of its wildlife found nowhere else — lemurs, baobabs, the spiny forest of the south, and rainforest along the eastern escarpment.

After the cyclone season, April to November, with whales in July and August.

Explore Madagascar →

The dry season · the long walk to water

Some of this still happens at the scale it always did.

When the pans go down the herds walk — tens of kilometres in a day, along routes that are older than any border drawn over them, until the rains turn them back. Nobody arranges it and nobody can promise it. You are either standing there when it happens or you plan a year around being.

Your journey can start long before you leave.

The best part of going to Africa is not the fortnight. It is the two years of knowing where you are going. Settle on the country, pick the month, and everything else — the passport, the season, the shape of the days — has time to be got right.
