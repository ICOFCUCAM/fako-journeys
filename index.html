Start where the forest is still breathing.

East Africa Uganda

Half the world's mountain gorillas, the source of the Nile, the Rwenzori snowline on the equator and savanna parks within a day's drive of each other.

Not the Serengeti first. Half the world's mountain gorillas live on three volcanoes, and you walk to them.

East Africa Ethiopia

The only African country never colonised, with its own alphabet, calendar and church — rock-hewn churches at Lalibela, the Simien escarpment above four thousand metres, and the Danakil below sea level.

Eighty per cent of Africa above three thousand metres is in one country, and the Simien escarpment has gelada on it.

Explore Ethiopia →

Go west of the famous forests.

Central & West Africa Cameroon

Six ecological zones in nine hundred kilometres: an active 4,095 m volcano, Atlantic black sand, Korup rainforest, the Grassfields kingdoms and the Sahel edge at Waza.

Korup is older than the Congo basin's and has a canopy walkway thirty metres up, with almost nobody on it.

Explore Cameroon →

Skip the resorts on the other coast.

Southern Africa Mozambique

The Indian Ocean coast of southern Africa — the Bazaruto and Quirimbas archipelagos, Ilha de Moçambique, Gorongosa's restored wildlife, and a Portuguese-Swahili culture that exists nowhere else.

Two thousand kilometres of Indian Ocean, most of it still without a road, and dhows instead of speedboats.

Explore Mozambique →

Begin where the coast remembers.

West Africa Ghana

The most straightforward country in West Africa to travel, with the Atlantic slave forts of the coast, the Ashanti kingdom at Kumasi, Mole's elephants in the north, and a music scene worth planning around.

Cape Coast and Elmina are kept as they were, the Ashanti court still sits at Kumasi, and there is a band on every night.

Explore Ghana →

Eat where the bread is still communal.

North Africa Morocco

Four thousand metres of High Atlas within three hours of the Atlantic, four imperial cities, and the edge of the Sahara at Merzouga — a country you can cross from surf to dunes in two days.

Tagine cooked slowly, bread baked in the neighbourhood oven, and a souk that sells to the people who live there.

Explore Morocco →

The oldest journey there is.

North Africa Egypt

The oldest continuous tourism in the world, and still the reason most people first think of Africa — the Giza plateau, the Valley of the Kings, Abu Simbel, and the Red Sea reefs on the other coast.

People have travelled to see this for two and a half thousand years, and the Valley of the Kings is still painted floor to ceiling.

Explore Egypt →

The best self-drive country on the continent: gravel, dunes, and the darkest measured night sky anywhere.

01The scale

Africa is bigger than the map in your head.

Every world map most people have ever looked at is Mercator, which inflates everything away from the equator and shrinks everything on it. Lay the countries you already have a sense of inside the real outline and the argument for starting with a region rather than a continent makes itself.

30.4million km²

All four together come to 26.6 million. Africa is 30.4. These are drawn with the same equal-area projection as the map above, each centred on itself — so this is geometry, not an illustration of a fact.

Ghana

The most straightforward country in West Africa to travel, with the Atlantic slave forts of the coast, the Ashanti kingdom at Kumasi, Mole's elephants in the north, and a music scene worth planning around.

Nigeria

One in five Africans is Nigerian. Afrobeats, Nollywood, the Benin bronzes, Yankari's elephants and the Obudu plateau — a country that exports its culture faster than anywhere else on earth.

Dry season across the country, and the festival calendar sits inside it.

Senegal

The westernmost point of Africa, with Dakar on its peninsula, Gorée offshore, the Sine-Saloum delta, the Djoudj bird sanctuary and a music tradition that shaped a good deal of what came after.

Dry, and cool enough on the coast until the heat arrives in June.

Rwanda

Mountain gorillas on the Virunga volcanoes, a canopy walk over Nyungwe, Lake Kivu, and a capital that is among the cleanest and safest in Africa.

The Serengeti and the Ngorongoro crater, the highest mountain in Africa, and a spice island with a thousand-year-old stone town on it.

Botswana

The Okavango empties into the Kalahari and evaporates, and the whole country is organised around that fact — low-volume, high-value safari, mokoro channels, and the salt pans of Makgadikgadi.

The Okavango flood peaks between June and August, months after the rain that made it.

The Indian Ocean coast of southern Africa — the Bazaruto and Quirimbas archipelagos, Ilha de Moçambique, Gorongosa's restored wildlife, and a Portuguese-Swahili culture that exists nowhere else.

Dry, clear and calm on the coast from April to October.

South Africa

Table Mountain over Cape Town, Big Five in the Kruger lowveld, the Drakensberg escarpment, two oceans meeting, and eleven official languages.

Year-round, but the Cape is a summer place and Kruger a winter one.

Zambia

Victoria Falls from the side that gets wet, the walking safari where it was invented, the Lower Zambezi by canoe, and parks with almost no traffic in them.

Walking safaris run in the dry season; many camps close for the rains.

Zimbabwe

Victoria Falls on one border, Great Zimbabwe's stone city in the interior, Hwange's elephant herds and Mana Pools — the park where the walking safari was refined into an art.

The falls are fullest in April; the game viewing is best from August.

North Africa

Egypt

The oldest continuous tourism in the world, and still the reason most people first think of Africa — the Giza plateau, the Valley of the Kings, Abu Simbel, and the Red Sea reefs on the other coast.

The cool half of the year. Upper Egypt in August is not a holiday.

Explore Egypt →

Morocco

Four thousand metres of High Atlas within three hours of the Atlantic, four imperial cities, and the edge of the Sahara at Merzouga — a country you can cross from surf to dunes in two days.

Spring and autumn: the Atlas is walkable and the south is not yet an oven.

Explore Morocco →

Tunisia

Carthage on the bay of Tunis, Roman Dougga and El Jem, the Sahara at Douz and Tozeur, and a Mediterranean coast that has been in the tourism business for sixty years.

Spring and early autumn, either side of a Mediterranean summer.

Explore Tunisia →

Islands

Madagascar

Separated from Africa 160 million years ago, with ninety per cent of its wildlife found nowhere else — lemurs, baobabs, the spiny forest of the south, and rainforest along the eastern escarpment.

After the cyclone season, April to November, with whales in July and August.

Explore Madagascar →

Mauritius

A volcanic island inside an almost unbroken reef, with a mountainous interior, Black River Gorges, and a culture assembled from India, Africa, France and China in the space of two centuries.

The dry winter, and the cyclone season is January to March.

Explore Mauritius →

Seychelles

The only granite islands in the middle of an ocean anywhere on earth — Mahé, Praslin and La Digue, the coco de mer in the Vallée de Mai, and Aldabra's giant tortoises a thousand kilometres out.

Warm all year; April, May, October and November are the calm months between trade winds.

Explore Seychelles →
