01Highlights

What Ghana is known for.

Six of the twenty-seven, and the ones most people come for. The rest are below — and none of them is filler.

Nature & Landscapes

Coast to Savanna

Rainforest and lagoon in the south, transitional woodland through the middle, savanna at Mole.

Wildlife

Mole and Ankasa

Elephants that walk into the camp waterhole at Mole, and forest elephants at Ankasa.

Mountains

The Akwapim and Afadja

Afadjato is 885 metres and the highest point, walkable in a morning from Liati Wote.

Beaches & Coast

Busua and Cape Three Points

Surf at Busua, and the point where the country runs out into the Gulf of Guinea.

Forests & Rainforest

Kakum

Rainforest with a canopy walkway thirty metres up, strung between seven emergent trees.

Culture

Ashanti

A kingdom that fought the British to a standstill, and still keeps its own court at Kumasi.

02Experiences

25 ways to spend the time.

Every country we cover works through the same twenty-seven categories, so you can hold two of them side by side and compare like with like rather than one brochure against another. Compare Ghana with another → The two not listed here are the hero picture and the case for going, which has a section of its own below.

Landscapes
  • Coast to SavannaRainforest and lagoon in the south, transitional woodland through the middle, savanna at Mole.
  • The Akwapim and AfadjaAfadjato is 885 metres and the highest point, walkable in a morning from Liati Wote.
  • WliThe highest falls in West Africa, dropping out of forest on the Togo border, with bats overhead.
  • Lake VoltaThe largest reservoir on earth by surface area, with ferries that take a full day to cross it.
  • Busua and Cape Three PointsSurf at Busua, and the point where the country runs out into the Gulf of Guinea.
  • KakumRainforest with a canopy walkway thirty metres up, strung between seven emergent trees.
Wildlife
  • Mole and AnkasaElephants that walk into the camp waterhole at Mole, and forest elephants at Ankasa.
  • Walking Safari at MoleOne of the few parks in West Africa where the game drive is done on foot, with an armed ranger.
  • Community ForestThe Wechiau hippo sanctuary and the Boabeng-Fiema monkey sanctuary, both village-run.
  • Surf, Hike, PaddleBusua's breaks, the Volta hills, and canoes on the lake at Dodi.
People and culture
  • AshantiA kingdom that fought the British to a standstill, and still keeps its own court at Kumasi.
  • The Ewe and the DagombaKente weaving in Volta and Ashanti, and the Dagbon drumming traditions of the north.
  • Homowo and Chale WoteHomowo in Accra in August, and the Chale Wote street art festival in Jamestown.
  • Kente and AdinkraKente woven in strips at Bonwire, and adinkra symbols stamped by hand at Ntonso.
  • Jollof, Banku and Grilled TilapiaThe jollof argument is unwinnable; the tilapia off the grill with banku settles it.
  • Easy First AfricaEnglish-speaking, short distances, and about as gentle an introduction as the continent offers.
  • Chop Bars and TrotrosThe country runs on shared minibuses and roadside kitchens, and both are worth using.
Places
  • Accra and KumasiAccra on the Atlantic, and Kumasi's Kejetia — one of the largest markets in West Africa.
  • Posuban and Colonial CoastThe Fante posuban shrines, and the trading architecture strung along the coast road.
  • The Slave FortsCape Coast and Elmina — the doors of no return, kept as they were and not softened.
  • The Year of ReturnGhana asked the diaspora to come back, and a great many people did.
Ways to travel
  • Canopy, Surf and LakeThe Kakum walkway, Busua's beach breaks, and hiking the Volta hills to the Togo border.
  • Lodges on the WaterSmall lodges above the beach at Busua and Axim, and the Mole escarpment rooms over the waterhole.
  • Colour EverywhereKente, market cloth and painted trotros — nothing here is any colour by accident.
  • NzulezoA village built entirely on stilts over Lake Tandane, reached only by canoe.
