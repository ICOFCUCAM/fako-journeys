Landscapes
  • Mangrove to SahelNiger delta mangrove in the south, rainforest, savanna, and the Sahel at the Niger border.
  • The Obudu PlateauSixteen hundred metres of grassland on the Cameroon border, with cattle ranches and cloud.
  • Erin-Ijesha and GuraraErin-Ijesha's seven stages in Osun, and the Gurara falls on the road to Abuja.
  • The Niger and the BenueTwo great rivers meeting at Lokoja, and the delta they build a thousand kilometres downstream.
  • Lagos and the AtlanticTarkwa Bay, Elegushi and the long Atlantic beaches east towards Lekki.
  • Cross RiverThe Cross River forest holds the last of Nigeria's gorillas and some of West Africa's tallest trees.
Wildlife
  • Yankari and GashakaYankari's elephant herds, and Gashaka-Gumti's forest chimpanzees on the Cameroon border.
  • YankariThe country's best game reserve, with a warm spring you can swim in at the centre of it.
  • Community ForestAfi Mountain's drill ranch and the Cross River community forests, both village-run.
  • Hike, Paddle, RideThe Jos plateau, the Obudu ranch trails, and creek boats through the delta.
People and culture
  • Five Hundred LanguagesYoruba, Igbo, Hausa and around five hundred more, in one country.
  • Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, FulaniFour large cultures and hundreds of smaller ones, each with its own courts and calendars.
  • Osun-Osogbo and CalabarThe Osun-Osogbo grove festival in August, and the Calabar carnival through December.
  • Bronze, Adire and Aso OkeBenin bronze casting still practised in Igun street, and adire resist-dyed cloth in Abeokuta.
  • Jollof, Suya and EgusiSuya off the grill at night, egusi with pounded yam, and the jollof question again.
  • Loud, and WelcomingNot a quiet holiday — but there is nowhere friendlier once you are through the door.
  • Danfo and BukaThe yellow buses, the roadside buka, and a city that does not stop at any hour.
Places
  • Lagos, Abuja, KanoLagos on the lagoon, purpose-built Abuja in the middle, and Kano's thousand-year-old walls.
  • Mud Mosques and ModernismHausa mud architecture in the north, and the 1970s modernism of Lagos and Abuja.
  • Benin City and Sungbo's EredoThe walls of Benin, and Sungbo's Eredo — a hundred and sixty kilometres of earthworks.
  • The BronzesThe Benin bronzes are the reason half the museums of Europe have a Nigeria problem.
Ways to travel
  • Plateau and DeltaHiking the Jos plateau, the Obudu cable car, and creek trips through the delta.
  • Lagos Island HotelsVictoria Island and Ikoyi at one end, and Obudu's mountain resort at the other.
  • Colour and CrowdMarkets, festivals and cloth — the hardest thing here is choosing what to leave out.
  • IdanreThe Idanre hills, with an old town six hundred steps up and nobody else climbing them.
