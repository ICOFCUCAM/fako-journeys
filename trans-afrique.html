The idea

Africa is too large to be a destination.

Dakar to Mombasa is further than Lisbon to Kabul, and there are fifty-four countries in between. A week in one of them is a week in one of them — which is the right journey for most people most of the time, and it is what the rest of this site is for.

A crossing is the other answer. You travel by road, you wake up in a different country most weeks, and the continent changes slowly enough to watch it happen: the landscape first, then the language, then what is for sale at the roadside, then what is for dinner. Nobody assembles that from home. Borders keep their own hours, permits are booked in months rather than days, and the part that decides whether a month works is having somebody whose job is the phone call at the frontier post on a Sunday.

About medical support

On a long expedition a medical professional can accompany the group, with the level of support set by the itinerary, the group size and the destination. It is confirmed for your crossing before you book and never assumed.

It is additional expedition support and not a replacement for travel and medical insurance or for local emergency services. Every traveller carries their own insurance; that requirement does not change because a doctor is on the crossing.
