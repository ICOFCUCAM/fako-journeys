Angola

The Kalandula falls, and a coast nobody has seen.

Kalandula is one of the largest waterfalls in Africa by volume, the Namib runs up the whole southern coast to the Iona dunes, and Luanda's bay is closed by a sand spit with the Atlantic on the other side of it.

  • Kissama and Luengue-LuianaKissama two hours from Luanda, restocked and recovering; the southeast on the Okavango's headwaters.

  • The Escarpment RoadsSerra da Leba's hairpins, and Tundavala where the plateau simply stops.
People and culture
  • Semba, Kizomba and KuduroSemba became samba across the Atlantic, and kuduro went back the other way.
  • Ovimbundu, Mbundu, Bakongo, OvahimbaOvimbundu in the centre, Bakongo in the north, and Ovahimba along the Cunene.
  • Carnaval da VitoriaLuanda's carnival on the Marginal, with the neighbourhood groups competing.
  • The Pensador and BasketryThe Chokwe Thinker is the national symbol, and Chokwe masks and chairs are among Africa's finest.
  • Funge, Muamba and CaluluFunge with muamba de galinha, calulu with dried fish, and Portuguese pastries after.
  • The MussuloA lagoon behind the spit south of Luanda, calm, shallow and full of families at weekends.
  • The Marginal at SixThe whole city walks and runs the bay road in the evening, and keeps doing it after dark.
Places
  • Luanda and BenguelaA bay closed by a sand spit and a skyline that went up fast, and a quieter colonial port south.
  • Portuguese Colonial and ModernistFortaleza de Sao Miguel, Benguela's pastel streets, and Luanda's late-modernist blocks.
  • Mbanza KongoThe capital of the Kingdom of Kongo, and the first sub-Saharan city to have a stone cathedral.
  • Mbanza Kongo, ListedInscribed in 2017 as the seat of a kingdom that dealt with Portugal as an equal.
