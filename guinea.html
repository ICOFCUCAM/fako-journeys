Guinea

The water tower of West Africa.

The Niger, the Senegal and the Gambia all rise in the Fouta Djallon, a sandstone highland of waterfalls and cliffs — and Conakry has produced more of the region's music than anywhere except Bamako.

02Experiences

25 ways to spend the time.

Every country we cover works through the same twenty-seven categories, so you can hold two of them side by side and compare like with like rather than one brochure against another. Compare Guinea with another → The two not listed here are the hero picture and the case for going, which has a section of its own below.

Landscapes
  • Where the Rivers RiseThe Niger, the Senegal and the Gambia all begin within a hundred kilometres of each other.
  • Mount Nimba1,752 metres on the Liberian border, with species that live on that mountain and nowhere else.
  • Ditinn and KinkonDitinn drops a hundred metres off a sheer cliff; Kinkon runs through a gorge below Pita.
  • The Upper NigerIt rises near the Sierra Leone border and is already wide by the time it leaves the country.
  • Three Hundred Kilometres of AtlanticMangrove coast with the Iles de Los off Conakry, and beaches on Roume and Kassa.
  • The Guinean ForestZiama and Diecke in the southeast — some of the last high forest in West Africa.
Wildlife
  • Chimpanzees at BossouOne of the best-studied wild chimpanzee groups anywhere, and one that uses stone tools.
  • The Forest, Not the PlainThere is no savanna circuit. What Guinea has is forest primates and the Nimba massif.
  • Nimba's EndemicsThe mountain has species found on it and nowhere else, and an iron deposit under them.
  • Trekking the HighlandsThe Fouta has marked routes, village guides and huts, which is rare in the region.
People and culture
  • Where the Music Came FromBembeya Jazz, the Ballets Africains, and a state that funded orchestras for two decades.
  • Fulani, Malinke, SusuFulani in the Fouta, Malinke in the upper Niger country, Susu on the coast.
  • Masked Dance in the Forest RegionThe forest peoples of the southeast keep mask societies that are not for visitors, and some that are.
  • Weaving and IndigoFouta indigo cloth, Malinke weaving, and the leatherwork of the highland towns.
  • Riz Gras and FoutiRice with palm oil and fish, and the sour milk and maize of the Fouta.
  • Waterfalls You Can Swim InMost of the Fouta's falls have a pool at the bottom and a path down to it.
  • Market Day in LabeThe highland market fills with Fulani from every village within a day's walk.
Places
  • ConakryA capital on a peninsula, with the Grande Mosquee and the sea on both sides.
  • Colonial and Fouta VernacularFrench colonial in Conakry, and round thatched houses with painted walls in the highlands.
  • The Almamy's FoutaThe Fouta Djallon was a theocratic Fulani state for a century and a half before the French came.
  • Nimba, ListedMount Nimba is a World Heritage site for its endemic toad, viviparous, that lives on the ridge.
Ways to travel
  • Hiking the FoutaVillage to village between waterfalls and cliffs, with the best walking network in the region.
  • ModestHotels in Conakry and a few in Labe and Dalaba. The Fouta runs on homestays.
  • Cliffs and WaterThe Fouta escarpments in the hour after the rain stops, when everything is running.
  • The Dame de MaliA rock face near Mali-ville shaped like a woman's profile, at the top of the Fouta.
