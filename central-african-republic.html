Central African Republic

Dzanga Bai, and the forest elephants.

Dzanga Bai is a forest clearing where a hundred elephants come to drink mineral water in a single afternoon, and Dzanga-Sangha holds western lowland gorillas habituated for tracking — one of the great wildlife experiences on earth, in one of the hardest countries to reach.

Landscapes
  • The Congo Basin ForestThe northern edge of the second largest rainforest on earth, and it starts here.
  • The Fertit Hills and Mont Ngaoui1,420 metres on the Cameroon border, in country almost nobody visits.
  • BoaliFifty metres of water on the M'Bali river, two hours from Bangui.
  • The Sangha and the UbangiThe Sangha runs down to the Congo; the Ubangi is the whole southern border.
  • River SandLandlocked. The sandbanks on the Sangha come out in the dry season and people use them.
  • Dzanga-SanghaOld-growth Congo Basin forest, shared with Cameroon and Congo as a trinational park.
Wildlife
  • Western Lowland GorillasHabituated groups tracked on foot, with far fewer visitors than in the east.
  • Forest, Not SavannaThis is bai-watching and forest tracking. Bongo, forest buffalo and sitatunga, all on foot.
  • Dzanga-Sangha's ModelThe reserve employs BaAka trackers and shares gate revenue with the villages around it.
  • Forest WalkingEverything here is done on foot, quietly, behind a tracker.
People and culture
  • The BaAkaForest hunter-gatherers whose polyphonic singing is on the UNESCO intangible list.
  • BaAka and Sangha-SanghaThe BaAka in the forest, and the Sangha-Sangha along the river.
  • BaAka PolyphonySung in overlapping parts, with no leader and no fixed melody, and it has been studied by composers for fifty years.
  • Nets, Baskets and Bark ClothHunting nets woven from forest liana, and bark cloth beaten from tree fibre.
  • Cassava, Bushmeat and Palm WineCassava as the staple, smoked fish from the river, and palm wine tapped in the forest.
  • Not YetThis is expedition travel in a country with a hard recent history, and it is not for families.
  • The Marche CentralEverything from cassava to river fish, and the noise of a city that never stops trading.
Places
  • BanguiOn the Ubangi, opposite the Congo, with the Marche Central at the middle of it.
  • Colonial and Forest CampFrench colonial in Bangui, and leaf-and-sapling mongulu shelters in the forest.
  • Bangui's Colonial QuarterA river capital laid out by the French, and much of it still standing.
  • Two SitesDzanga-Sangha for its forest, and Manovo-Gounda St Floris in the north, on the danger list.
Ways to travel
  • Tracking on FootGorillas in the morning, the bai in the afternoon, and net-hunting with the BaAka between.
  • One LodgeSangha Lodge at Dzanga-Sangha, and nothing comparable anywhere else in the country.
  • Elephants in a ClearingDzanga Bai from the platform at four in the afternoon, when the light comes in sideways.
  • Chimpanzees at Bai HokouA second habituated group, and the walk in is as good as the sighting.
