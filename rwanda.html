Terraces to the Horizon

Almost every hillside in Rwanda is farmed in contour terraces — the landscape is entirely made by hand.

Wildlife

25 ways to spend the time.

Landscapes
  • Terraces to the HorizonAlmost every hillside in Rwanda is farmed in contour terraces — the landscape is entirely made by hand.
  • The Virunga VolcanoesFive cones along the border — Karisimbi, Bisoke, Muhabura — with a crater lake at the top of Bisoke.
  • Isumo, Under NyungweA steep tea-and-forest walk down to the Isumo falls on the western edge of the Nyungwe canopy.
  • Lake KivuOne of Africa's great lakes, with no bilharzia, no crocodiles, and the Congo Nile Trail along its shore.
  • Rubavu LakeshoreSand, palms and calm freshwater at Gisenyi, an hour from the gorilla trailheads.
  • The Nyungwe Canopy WalkA suspension walkway sixty metres above one of Africa's oldest montane rainforests, with thirteen primate species below it.
Wildlife
  • Face to Face with GorillasTen habituated families on the Virunga slopes, one hour with them, eight people at a time.
  • Akagera, Big Five AgainLion returned in 2015, rhino in 2017; the eastern savanna park now has all five, twenty years after losing them.
  • Ten Percent, Back to the HillsA share of every permit goes to the communities bordering the parks, and gorilla numbers have climbed since it started.
  • Bisoke Crater LakeThree hours up through nettles and bamboo to a crater lake at 3,711 m, and three hours back down through mud.
People and culture
  • Intore, the Dance of HeroesThe Intore warriors' dance, the inanga zither, and drumming that was once the property of the king alone.
  • Living MemoryCultural villages near Musanze where former poachers now guide, and the beekeepers and potters of the Virunga foothills.
  • Kwita IzinaThe annual gorilla naming ceremony at Kinigi, where every infant born that year is named in front of the whole district.
  • Imigongo and AgasekeGeometric imigongo panels in black, white and ochre, and the woven agaseke peace basket that is on the coat of arms.
  • Brochettes and IsombeGrilled goat brochettes, cassava leaf isombe, tilapia from Kivu, and some of the best coffee grown anywhere.
  • UmugandaThe last Saturday of every month, the country stops and works on something communal. Visitors are welcome to join.
  • Kimironko MarketFabric, fruit and everything else under one roof, and the tailors who will make it up by tomorrow.
Places
  • Kigali, Across the RidgesBuilt along ridge lines, swept every morning, and with a monthly Umuganda when the whole city cleans it together.
  • The Convention Centre DomeThe Kigali Convention Centre dome, lit at night in the national colours, above a city of red-roofed ridges.
  • Nyanza, the King's PalaceThe reconstructed royal residence at Nyanza, thatched and enormous, with the long-horned Inyambo cattle still kept.
  • Memory and ReconstructionThe Kigali Genocide Memorial is where any honest visit to Rwanda starts, and it explains everything that came after.
Ways to travel
  • The Congo Nile Trail227 kilometres along Lake Kivu on foot or by bike, through coffee terraces and fishing villages.
  • Lodges Below the VolcanoesA handful of very good lodges within sight of the park boundary, built so the trek starts after breakfast.
  • Mist on the VolcanoesThe Virunga chain clears for about an hour after dawn, and the terraces below hold the light for longer.
  • Gishwati-MukuraRwanda's newest national park, a recovering forest between Nyungwe and the volcanoes that almost nobody visits.
