02Booking process

From first email to the vehicle at your hotel.

Five steps, and you can stop at any of the first three without owing us anything.

01

You write, we answer

Month, rough dates, how many of you and what you want to see. We come back within one working day with a suggested circuit and an honest view of whether the season suits it.

02

Written quote and day-by-day

One page of itinerary, one page of price in FCFA and euro with the park fees itemised. Revise it as many times as you like — most bookings go through three or four versions.

03

Provisional hold

We block guides, vehicle and rooms for seven days at no cost while you check flights and leave. Internal flights are the one thing we cannot hold without payment.

04

Deposit and confirmation

Twenty-five per cent confirms it. You receive the full terms, the insurance requirement, the kit list and the name and telephone number of your guide.

05

Briefing, then departure

Balance at 45 days. A briefing the evening before — in person in Douala or Buea, or by video call — and then the vehicle at your hotel at the hour written on the itinerary.

03Season by season

Book the country to the calendar, not the other way round.

Cameroon does not have one season, it has several running against each other. Tell us your month and we will tell you which half of the country to spend it in.

Nov – FebEverything open

Dry in both halves. Trekking, coast, highlands and north all work. Our busiest period; the mountain huts and the February Race of Hope week go first.

Mar – MayNorth peaks, coast turns

The best weeks at Waza and Bénoué, and hot with it. The south-west begins to break by late April and the mountain closes toward the end of May.

Jun – SepThe Buea rains

Serious rain in the south-west — the wettest month at Debundscha can pass two metres. No summit treks. Highlands, Foumban and Yaoundé run normally and cost less.

OctThe quiet turn

Rain easing, everything green, almost nobody travelling. Excellent for the Ring Road and Foumban, marginal for the mountain, too early for the north.

04Before you write

The questions that arrive every week.

If yours is not here, telephone. Neither office minds a long question.

Do we need a visa?

Almost every nationality does. The e-visa is issued online before travel and we send the invitation and confirmed itinerary the application asks for as soon as your deposit clears.

Is yellow fever vaccination required?

Yes, and the certificate is checked on arrival at Douala. Antimalarials are strongly advised for every region and essential in the north.

How fit do we need to be for the mountain?

Fit enough to walk nine hours uphill on a rough path two days running. No technical skill and no rope work, but it is a long way up and the altitude is real above 3,000 metres.

Can you take a single traveller?

On fixed departures, yes, at the group rate. Privately, we can run for one, though the per-person figure is close to the two-traveller price for the whole vehicle.

What about mobile signal and cash?

MTN and Orange cover most of the route. There is no signal on the mountain above Hut 1 and none in Korup. Carry cash for the north — the last dependable machine is in Maroua.

Do you take regional travellers?

Constantly. CEMAC and Cameroonian residents pay the resident park tariff, and we run short weekend departures out of Douala and Yaoundé through the dry season.
