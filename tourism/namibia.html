Where the Desert Meets the Wild

The oldest desert on earth running into the Atlantic — Sossusvlei's dunes, the Skeleton Coast, Etosha's waterholes and Damaraland's desert elephant, across a country with almost nobody in it.

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Namib

Fifty-five million years old, and dunes at Sossusvlei three hundred metres high.

Photo Hub JACQU / Pexels

The Brandberg and the Escarpment

The Brandberg is 2,573 metres and holds the White Lady, one of Africa's known rock paintings.

Photo Hc Digital / Pexels

Epupa

The Kunene drops through a gorge on the Angolan border, with baobabs on the rim.

Photo Arno Smit / Unsplash

The Kunene and the Zambezi

Two permanent rivers, one on each border, and dry riverbeds for everything in between.

Photo Eelco Böhtlingk / Unsplash

The Skeleton Coast

Fifteen hundred kilometres of Atlantic, fog, shipwrecks and seal colonies.

Photo JP Desvigne / Unsplash

The Caprivi Woodland

No rainforest — the green is in the Zambezi region, riverine and full of elephant.

Photo Frank Cone / Pexels

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Desert-Adapted Wildlife

Elephant and rhino that walk sixty kilometres between water, and lion that hunt seals.

Photo Rino Adamo / Pexels

Etosha

Game that comes to you: in the dry season everything in the park is at one of the waterholes.

Photo Rino Adamo / Pexels

Conservancies

Communal conservancies cover a fifth of the country and are why the black rhino survived.

Photo Faniry Raoilison / Pexels

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Eleven Peoples

Owambo, Herero, Damara, Nama, San and more, and German still on half the street signs.

Photo Khulani Faniso / Pexels

The Himba

The Himba of the Kunene, whose way of life is genuinely intact and should be visited carefully.

Photo Darkshade Photos / Pexels

Braai and Biltong

Game meat over coals, biltong on every counter, and German bread that is actually German.

Photo Ultimate Safaris Namibia / Unsplash

Windhoek Karnival and Maherero Day

The Windhoek carnival in April, and Maherero Day at Okahandja in August.

Photo Skylight Views / Pexels

Basketry and Ostrich Shell

Kavango wood carving, San ostrich-eggshell beadwork, and Herero doll making.

Photo Chris F / Pexels

Twyfelfontein

Two thousand rock engravings on one hillside, cut into sandstone six thousand years ago.

Photo Markus Kammermann / Unsplash

Kolmanskop and the Coast

A diamond town the desert has taken back, and the German colonial coast at Lüderitz.

Photo Taylor Wright / Unsplash

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Windhoek and Swakopmund

Windhoek on the highveld, and Swakopmund where German architecture meets Atlantic fog.

Photo Anqi Lu / Unsplash

Colonial and Desert Vernacular

German colonial in the towns, and camps built to disappear into the gravel plains.

Photo Arthur Souquet / Unsplash

The Long Road

Fuel, biltong and a farm stall every two hundred kilometres, and that is the day.

Photo Shane Kell / Pexels

Easy and Safe to Drive

Good roads, no malaria in most of it, and children who will remember the dunes.

Photo Joe McDaniel / Unsplash

Self-Drive Country

Climbing Big Daddy, quad biking at Swakopmund, and balloons over the Namib at dawn.
