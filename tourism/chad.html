Zakouma

Zakouma brought its elephants back from four hundred and fifty to over six hundred, the Ennedi is a sandstone massif full of rock art and desert crocodiles, and the Ounianga lakes sit in the middle of the Sahara without a river feeding them.

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Ennedi

A sandstone massif eroded into arches, towers and canyons, with water at the bottom of them.

Photo Djamel Ramdani / Pexels

Emi Koussi and the Tibesti

A 3,415-metre volcano in the far north, the highest point in the Sahara.

Photo Ben Khatry / Pexels

None, and Guelta Instead

No falls. What the Ennedi has is the Guelta d'Archei, a permanent gorge pool.

Photo Francesco Ungaro / Pexels

Lake Chad and the Ounianga Lakes

A shrinking inland sea in the west, and eighteen lakes in the Sahara that no river feeds.

Photo Moaz Tobok / Pexels

Landlocked, and the Lake

The beaches here are the shores of Lake Chad, worked by fishermen in reed boats.

Photo Anthony Beck / Pexels

Gallery Forest on the Rivers

The Chari and Logone carry woodland far north into the Sahel.

Photo kokoko cookiez / Pexels

Culture & heritage

Culture

Two Hundred Groups

Toubou and Zaghawa in the north, Sara in the south, and Arabic and French across both.

Photo The Chongaiman Ace / Pexels

The Toubou

A desert people of the Tibesti and Ennedi, and among the most self-sufficient anywhere.

Photo mg shotz / Pexels

Boule and Grilled Capitaine

Millet boule with sauce, and Nile perch off the Chari grilled on the bank.

The Gerewol and the Camel Races

Wodaabe gatherings in the Sahel, and camel racing in the desert north.

Photo Faruk Tokluoğlu / Pexels

Leatherwork and Silver

Toubou leather, Sara basketry, and silver from the N'Djamena market.

Ennedi Rock Art

Thousands of painted and engraved panels recording a Sahara with cattle and giraffe in it.

The Ennedi massif and the Ounianga lakes, both World Heritage, both barely visited.
