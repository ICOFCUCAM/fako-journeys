EQUATORIAL GUINEA · Tourism

Bioko

Bioko is a volcanic island with cloud forest, seven primate species and four nesting turtle species on its southern beaches; the mainland at Monte Alen holds gorillas and forest elephant.

Central & West AfricaRegion

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Gran Caldera

A collapsed volcanic crater in the south of Bioko, forested, and reached only on foot.

Pico Basile

3,011 metres, the island's volcano, with a road most of the way up it.

Iladyi

A 250-metre cascade off the caldera rim in the south of Bioko.

The Wele and the Muni

The Wele runs across the mainland; the Muni estuary marks the Gabonese border.

Black Sand and Turtles

Four species nest on Bioko's southern beaches — leatherback, green, olive ridley and hawksbill.

Cloud Forest and Lowland

Cloud forest above a thousand metres on Bioko, and Congo Basin forest on the mainland.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Seven Primates on One Island

Bioko has seven species, several of them found on that island and nowhere else.

Monte Alen

Mainland forest with gorilla, chimpanzee and forest elephant, walked rather than driven.

The Bioko Biodiversity Programme

University-run monitoring of the turtle beaches and primates since 1998.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Spanish, Fang and Bubi

The only Spanish-speaking country in Africa, with Fang on the mainland and Bubi on Bioko.

The Bubi

Bioko's first people, with their own language, and a history separate from the mainland.

Pepe Soup and Malamba

Pepper soup with fish, and malamba — palm wine sweetened with cane.

The Abira

A Bubi purification ceremony, danced with drums and rattles.

Carving and Basketry

Fang carving on the mainland, and Bubi basketry and wooden bells on Bioko.

Colonial Malabo

Santa Isabel was a Spanish port town, and the cathedral and customs house still stand.

Nothing Listed Yet

No World Heritage sites, and a colonial architecture that has been very little studied.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Malabo and Bata

The island capital in a drowned crater, and the mainland's largest city on the coast.

Spanish Colonial

Malabo's cathedral, customs house and shuttered merchant houses, in a Gulf of Guinea version of Spain.

Sunday After Mass

Malabo empties into the streets after the cathedral, and stays there.

Beach and Forest

Arena Blanca on Bioko is calm and shaded, and Pico Basile is drivable.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Trekking the Caldera

Three days on foot from Ureca to the Gran Caldera, through forest, with no road at all.

Walking Bioko

The south of the island has no roads. Everything there is done on foot.

Business Hotels, Mostly

Malabo and Bata have good hotels built for the oil industry, and Bioko's south has none.

Black Sand and Green

Ureca in the rain, which is most of the time — the wettest place in Africa.

Annobon

A volcanic island six hundred kilometres out, with a crater lake and four flights a month.
