Leptis Magna

The best-preserved Roman city on the Mediterranean, Greek Cyrene on its escarpment, and the Ubari lakes in a sand sea — a country whose archaeology is world-class and whose access is not simple.

North AfricaRegion
27Tourism experiences
Leptis Magna, and the sand sea behind itKnown for
01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Sand Sea and the Jebel

The Ubari sand sea in the southwest, and the green Jebel Akhdar behind Benghazi.

The Acacus

Volcanic rock arches and towers on the Algerian border, with rock art under them.

Wadi Country

No permanent rivers. The water is in wadis that run once a decade and in springs at the oases.

The Ubari Lakes

Salt lakes between dunes, the last of them dense enough to float in.

Two Thousand Kilometres of Coast

The longest Mediterranean coastline of any African country, and almost none of it built on.

The Green Mountain

Juniper and cypress on the Jebel Akhdar, the only real forest in the country.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Desert Survivors

Barbary sheep in the Acacus, dorcas gazelle on the gravel plains, and very little else.

Tracking, Not Driving

The wildlife is thin and shy. What you go out to see is the country it survives in.

What the Desert Keeps

Sand buried Leptis Magna for a thousand years, which is why there is so much of it left.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Arab, Berber, Tuareg, Tebu

Four peoples and three languages, and a coast that has been Phoenician, Greek, Roman, Ottoman and Italian.

The Tuareg and the Tebu

The Tuareg in the southwest around Ghat, the Tebu in the Tibesti foothills in the southeast.

Bazin and Bsisa

Bazin with lamb and paprika, couscous on Fridays, and shorba on the table all Ramadan.

Ghat and the Desert Festivals

The Ghat festival brings Tuareg from three countries for racing, music and poetry.

Silver and Leather

Tuareg silver crosses, tooled leather from Ghadames, and Berber weaving in the Nafusa.

Cyrene and Sabratha

A Greek city on an escarpment above the sea, and a Roman theatre facing the water at Sabratha.

Five Sites, All of Them Major

Leptis, Sabratha, Cyrene, Ghadames and the Acacus rock art — every one on the World Heritage list.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Tripoli and Benghazi

An Ottoman medina inside Italian arcades in Tripoli, and Benghazi rebuilding.

Ghadames

A covered mud-brick town where the streets are tunnels and the roofs are the women's road.

Tea, Three Times

Poured from a height, three glasses, and no conversation is finished before the third.

Roman Cities Are Good for This

Leptis is enormous, empty and safe to run around in, which not every ruin is.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Into the Acacus

Four-wheel-drive expeditions of a week, camping under the arches with Tuareg guides.

The Jebel Akhdar

Walking on the green mountain between Greek tombs cut into the rock.

Thin on the Ground

There is very little at the top end here, and what exists is in Tripoli.

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Stone and Sea

Sabratha's theatre with the Mediterranean through its arches, in the last hour of light.

Waw an Namus

A black volcanic caldera in the middle of the Sahara with green lakes at the bottom.
