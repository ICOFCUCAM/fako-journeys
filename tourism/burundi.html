BURUNDI · Tourism

Hills and Lake

A country of hills above Lake Tanganyika, with the drummers of Gishora on the UNESCO list, chimpanzees at Kibira, and a marker at Rutovu on the ridge where the Nile's longest tributary begins.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Congo-Nile Divide

A ridge running the length of the country, with the Nile on one side and the Congo on the other.

Mount Heha

2,684 metres, an hour from the capital, with the whole rift visible from the top.

Karera

Four falls in one forested gorge in the southeast, with a path between them.

Lake Tanganyika

The second deepest lake on earth, and Burundi has a hundred and sixty kilometres of it.

Saga and Karera Beaches

White sand on Tanganyika south of Bujumbura, with the Congo visible across the water.

Kibira

Forty thousand hectares of montane forest on the divide, and the country's water tower.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Chimpanzees at Kibira

Montane forest continuous with Rwanda's Nyungwe, with chimpanzee and colobus in it.

Rusizi and Ruvubu

Hippo and crocodile in the Rusizi delta, and buffalo and antelope along the Ruvubu.

Kibira's Water

The forest feeds the hydro that runs most of the country, and protecting it is not sentiment.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

The Drums

The royal drummers of Gishora are on the UNESCO list, and they are not a performance troupe.

Hutu, Tutsi and Twa

One language, Kirundi, spoken by everybody, and a history the country is still working through.

Beans, Ugali and Mukeke

Beans and plantain, ugali, and mukeke — a Tanganyika fish grilled on the beach.

Gishora

The drum sanctuary above Gitega, where the drums are kept and danced on their own ground.

Basketry and Drums

Coiled agaseke baskets with pointed lids, and the drums themselves.

Rutovu

A stone pyramid on a ridge marking the southernmost source of the Nile.

The Ritual Dance of the Royal Drum

Inscribed in 2014 — the drums, the dance and the making of them, together.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Bujumbura and Gitega

A lakeside city with a long promenade, and the political capital up in the hills.

Colonial and Hillside

German and Belgian colonial in Bujumbura, and tiled hillside houses everywhere else.

The Beach on a Sunday

Bujumbura goes to Saga beach, and stays until the light goes.

Lake and Hills

Short distances, calm fresh water, and hippos to keep an eye on.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Walking the Divide

Kibira on foot, Mount Heha in a morning, and the lake shore road south.

Hiking the Hills

Heha, Kibira and the ridge roads, none of them more than a day.

Modest

A handful of hotels in Bujumbura, and lodges on the lake shore.

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Drums in Motion

Gishora mid-performance, when the drums are carried and struck at once.

Rusizi Delta

Hippos, birds and the Congo across the water, twenty minutes from the capital.
