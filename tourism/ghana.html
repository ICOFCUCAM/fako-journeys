GHANA · Tourism

The Forts, the Forest and the Highlife

The most straightforward country in West Africa to travel, with the Atlantic slave forts of the coast, the Ashanti kingdom at Kumasi, Mole's elephants in the north, and a music scene worth planning around.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Coast to Savanna

Rainforest and lagoon in the south, transitional woodland through the middle, savanna at Mole.

The Akwapim and Afadja

Afadjato is 885 metres and the highest point, walkable in a morning from Liati Wote.

Wli

The highest falls in West Africa, dropping out of forest on the Togo border, with bats overhead.

Lake Volta

The largest reservoir on earth by surface area, with ferries that take a full day to cross it.

Busua and Cape Three Points

Surf at Busua, and the point where the country runs out into the Gulf of Guinea.

Kakum

Rainforest with a canopy walkway thirty metres up, strung between seven emergent trees.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Mole and Ankasa

Elephants that walk into the camp waterhole at Mole, and forest elephants at Ankasa.

Walking Safari at Mole

One of the few parks in West Africa where the game drive is done on foot, with an armed ranger.

Community Forest

The Wechiau hippo sanctuary and the Boabeng-Fiema monkey sanctuary, both village-run.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Ashanti

A kingdom that fought the British to a standstill, and still keeps its own court at Kumasi.

The Ewe and the Dagomba

Kente weaving in Volta and Ashanti, and the Dagbon drumming traditions of the north.

Jollof, Banku and Grilled Tilapia

The jollof argument is unwinnable; the tilapia off the grill with banku settles it.

Homowo and Chale Wote

Homowo in Accra in August, and the Chale Wote street art festival in Jamestown.

Kente and Adinkra

Kente woven in strips at Bonwire, and adinkra symbols stamped by hand at Ntonso.

The Slave Forts

Cape Coast and Elmina — the doors of no return, kept as they were and not softened.

The Year of Return

Ghana asked the diaspora to come back, and a great many people did.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Accra and Kumasi

Accra on the Atlantic, and Kumasi's Kejetia — one of the largest markets in West Africa.

Posuban and Colonial Coast

The Fante posuban shrines, and the trading architecture strung along the coast road.

Chop Bars and Trotros

The country runs on shared minibuses and roadside kitchens, and both are worth using.

Easy First Africa

English-speaking, short distances, and about as gentle an introduction as the continent offers.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Canopy, Surf and Lake

The Kakum walkway, Busua's beach breaks, and hiking the Volta hills to the Togo border.

Surf, Hike, Paddle

Busua's breaks, the Volta hills, and canoes on the lake at Dodi.

Lodges on the Water

Small lodges above the beach at Busua and Axim, and the Mole escarpment rooms over the waterhole.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Colour Everywhere

Kente, market cloth and painted trotros — nothing here is any colour by accident.

Nzulezo

A village built entirely on stilts over Lake Tandane, reached only by canoe.
