CENTRAL AFRICAN REPUBLIC · Tourism

Dzanga Bai

Dzanga Bai is a forest clearing where a hundred elephants come to drink mineral water in a single afternoon, and Dzanga-Sangha holds western lowland gorillas habituated for tracking — one of the great wildlife experiences on earth, in one of the hardest countries to reach.

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Congo Basin Forest

The northern edge of the second largest rainforest on earth, and it starts here.

The Fertit Hills and Mont Ngaoui

1,420 metres on the Cameroon border, in country almost nobody visits.

Boali

Fifty metres of water on the M'Bali river, two hours from Bangui.

The Sangha and the Ubangi

The Sangha runs down to the Congo; the Ubangi is the whole southern border.

River Sand

Landlocked. The sandbanks on the Sangha come out in the dry season and people use them.

Dzanga-Sangha

Old-growth Congo Basin forest, shared with Cameroon and Congo as a trinational park.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Western Lowland Gorillas

Habituated groups tracked on foot, with far fewer visitors than in the east.

Forest, Not Savanna

This is bai-watching and forest tracking. Bongo, forest buffalo and sitatunga, all on foot.

Dzanga-Sangha's Model

The reserve employs BaAka trackers and shares gate revenue with the villages around it.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

The BaAka

Forest hunter-gatherers whose polyphonic singing is on the UNESCO intangible list.

BaAka and Sangha-Sangha

The BaAka in the forest, and the Sangha-Sangha along the river.

Cassava, Bushmeat and Palm Wine

Cassava as the staple, smoked fish from the river, and palm wine tapped in the forest.

BaAka Polyphony

Sung in overlapping parts, with no leader and no fixed melody, and it has been studied by composers for fifty years.

Nets, Baskets and Bark Cloth

Hunting nets woven from forest liana, and bark cloth beaten from tree fibre.

Bangui's Colonial Quarter

A river capital laid out by the French, and much of it still standing.

Two Sites

Dzanga-Sangha for its forest, and Manovo-Gounda St Floris in the north, on the danger list.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Bangui

On the Ubangi, opposite the Congo, with the Marche Central at the middle of it.

Colonial and Forest Camp

French colonial in Bangui, and leaf-and-sapling mongulu shelters in the forest.

The Marche Central

Everything from cassava to river fish, and the noise of a city that never stops trading.

Not Yet

This is expedition travel in a country with a hard recent history, and it is not for families.

05

Adventure & outdoors

Adventure

Tracking on Foot

Gorillas in the morning, the bai in the afternoon, and net-hunting with the BaAka between.

Forest Walking

Everything here is done on foot, quietly, behind a tracker.

One Lodge

Sangha Lodge at Dzanga-Sangha, and nothing comparable anywhere else in the country.
