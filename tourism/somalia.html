SOMALIA · Tourism

Three Thousand Kilometres

Three thousand kilometres of Indian Ocean, the painted rock shelters at Laas Geel that are among the oldest in Africa, and the Cal Madow mountains — a country with real destinations and real constraints on reaching them.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Cal Madow

A mountain range behind Bosaso with cloud forest on top and frankincense on the slopes.

Shimbiris

2,460 metres in the Cal Madow, the highest point, with juniper and cycads near the top.

Seasonal, and Sudden

The Shabelle and Juba run south; everything else runs for a fortnight after the rains.

The Juba and the Shabelle

Two rivers off the Ethiopian highlands that make the only irrigated farmland in the country.

Berbera and Beyond

Berbera's beaches on the Gulf of Aden, and a reef wall a short swim from the sand.

Frankincense Country

Boswellia trees on the Cal Madow slopes, tapped as they have been for four thousand years.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Speke's Gazelle and the Sea

Speke's gazelle and dibatag inland, and turtles, dugong and whale sharks offshore.

Not the Circuit

There are no game lodges. The wildlife here is coastal, and the interior is for those who know it.

Frankincense and the Land

Boswellia grows on bare limestone where nothing else will, and it holds the slope together.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Poetry as the National Art

Somalis are called a nation of poets, and the reputation is deserved and enforced.

Nomad and Pastoralist

Camel herding is the oldest way of life here and still a large part of the economy.

Camel Milk and Bariis

Camel milk, bariis iskukaris spiced with cumin and cardamom, and Somali tea with ginger.

Weddings and Buraanbur

Buraanbur — women's poetry with drumming and dance — carries the celebration.

Weaving and Woodcarving

Aqal nomadic house frames, carved wooden headrests, and woven mats from the north.

Laas Geel

Rock shelters near Hargeisa painted with cattle and people, five thousand years old and unfaded.

Ports of the Indian Ocean

Mogadishu, Merca and Barawa were trading cities when Europe had none on this ocean.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Mogadishu and Hargeisa

A white Indian Ocean capital rebuilding, and a highland city that has been quiet for thirty years.

Coral Stone and Carved Doors

Swahili coral-rag architecture along the southern coast, with heavy carved doors.

The Camel Market

Hargeisa's livestock market on a weekday morning, and the money that moves through it.

Beaches, and Care

Lido beach in Mogadishu fills at the weekend. Go with local advice, always.

05

Adventure & outdoors

Adventure

Diving Berbera

Unfished reefs in the Gulf of Aden, and almost no one to share them with.

Walking the Cal Madow

Cool forest, cycads and views to the Gulf of Aden, from Erigavo.

Beginning to Exist

New hotels in Hargeisa and Mogadishu, mostly for business travellers, some of them good.
