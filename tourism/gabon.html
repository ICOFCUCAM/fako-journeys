Loango

Eighty-eight per cent forest, thirteen national parks covering eleven per cent of the country, and at Loango forest elephant, buffalo and hippo come out onto an Atlantic beach — the only place on earth where that happens.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Eighty-Eight Per Cent Forest

One of the most forested countries on earth, and eleven per cent of it in national parks.

The Chaillu Massif

1,575 metres at Mont Iboundji, forested to the top, in the middle of the country.

Kongou and Mingouli

Kongou on the Ivindo — three kilometres wide in flood, in the middle of rainforest.

The Ogooue and the Ivindo

The Ogooue drains almost the whole country; the Ivindo runs through Ivindo National Park.

Eight Hundred Kilometres of Atlantic

Empty beaches with forest behind them, humpbacks offshore, and leatherbacks nesting.

Congo Basin Forest

Lope has one of the longest continuous records of forest-savanna change anywhere.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Lowland Gorillas and Mandrills

Habituated gorillas at Loango, and mandrill hordes at Lope that run to hundreds.

Forest and Savanna at Lope

Where the forest meets the last of the Ogooue savanna, with elephant and buffalo in both.

Thirteen Parks in One Decision

Gabon created all thirteen national parks in 2002, on one day, covering eleven per cent of the country.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Bwiti

An initiatory religion built around iboga, and the ceremonies run all night to harp and drum.

Fang, Punu, Myene and the Baka

Fang across the north, Punu in the south, and Baka forest people in the interior.

Nyembwe and Smoked Fish

Chicken in palm nut nyembwe sauce, and river fish smoked over a slow fire.

The Bwiti Night

Ngombi harp, mouth bow and dancing until dawn, and it is a religion rather than a show.

Masks and Ngombi Harps

Punu white-faced masks, Fang reliquary figures, and the carved harps of Bwiti.

Lope's Rock Art

Engravings on the Ogooue at Lope, and Iron Age sites going back two and a half thousand years.

Lope-Okanda, and Ivindo

Two World Heritage sites: the forest-savanna mosaic, and the falls on the Ivindo.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Libreville and Port-Gentil

An Atlantic capital, and an oil town on an island at the Ogooue delta.

Colonial and Contemporary

French colonial along the coast, and Libreville's newer buildings behind it.

Grilled Fish on the Sand

The Libreville beach restaurants at the weekend, and the fish landed that morning.

Elephants From a Boat

Loango's lagoon puts you at eye level with elephant and buffalo, safely, from the water.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Whales, Turtles and Surf

Humpbacks from July, leatherbacks from November, and a beach nobody else is on.

Kayak, Trek and Boat

Lagoon kayaking at Loango, forest trekking at Ivindo, and the train through Lope.

A Handful, and Good

Loango and Lope have real lodges, and the rest of the parks are camping.

Forest Meeting Ocean

Loango at low sun, when the elephants come out and the beach is empty in both directions.

The Trans-Gabonais

The railway from Libreville to Franceville, straight through Lope, overnight.
