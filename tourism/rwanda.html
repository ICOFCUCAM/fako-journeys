01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Terraces to the Horizon

Almost every hillside in Rwanda is farmed in contour terraces — the landscape is entirely made by hand.

Photo Quang Nguyen Vinh / Pexels

The Virunga Volcanoes

Five cones along the border — Karisimbi, Bisoke, Muhabura — with a crater lake at the top of Bisoke.

Isumo, Under Nyungwe

A steep tea-and-forest walk down to the Isumo falls on the western edge of the Nyungwe canopy.

Photo Valentina Restrepo Ríos / Pexels

Lake Kivu

One of Africa's great lakes, with no bilharzia, no crocodiles, and the Congo Nile Trail along its shore.

Photo Edouard MIHIGO / Pexels

Rubavu Lakeshore

Sand, palms and calm freshwater at Gisenyi, an hour from the gorilla trailheads.

Photo Mad Knoxx Deluxe / Pexels

The Nyungwe Canopy Walk

A suspension walkway sixty metres above one of Africa's oldest montane rainforests, with thirteen primate species below it.

Photo Juan Felipe Ramírez / Pexels

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Face to Face with Gorillas

Ten habituated families on the Virunga slopes, one hour with them, eight people at a time.

Akagera, Big Five Again

Lion returned in 2015, rhino in 2017; the eastern savanna park now has all five, twenty years after losing them.

Photo Derek Keats / Pexels

Ten Percent, Back to the Hills

A share of every permit goes to the communities bordering the parks, and gorilla numbers have climbed since it started.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Intore, the Dance of Heroes

The Intore warriors' dance, the inanga zither, and drumming that was once the property of the king alone.

Photo Ana Kenk / Pexels

Living Memory

Cultural villages near Musanze where former poachers now guide, and the beekeepers and potters of the Virunga foothills.

Photo From Salih / Pexels

Brochettes and Isombe

Grilled goat brochettes, cassava leaf isombe, tilapia from Kivu, and some of the best coffee grown anywhere.

Photo Ana Kenk / Pexels

Kwita Izina

The annual gorilla naming ceremony at Kinigi, where every infant born that year is named in front of the whole district.

Photo Ana Kenk / Pexels

Imigongo and Agaseke

Geometric imigongo panels in black, white and ochre, and the woven agaseke peace basket that is on the coat of arms.

Photo Diego F. Parra / Pexels

Nyanza, the King's Palace

The reconstructed royal residence at Nyanza, thatched and enormous, with the long-horned Inyambo cattle still kept.

Photo vasu dev / Pexels

Memory and Reconstruction

The Kigali Genocide Memorial is where any honest visit to Rwanda starts, and it explains everything that came after.

Photo Ana Kenk / Pexels

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Kigali, Across the Ridges

Built along ridge lines, swept every morning, and with a monthly Umuganda when the whole city cleans it together.

Photo Faustin NKURUNZIZA / Pexels

The Convention Centre Dome

The Kigali Convention Centre dome, lit at night in the national colours, above a city of red-roofed ridges.

Photo Faustin NKURUNZIZA / Pexels

Kimironko Market

Fabric, fruit and everything else under one roof, and the tailors who will make it up by tomorrow.

Photo Ana Kenk / Pexels

Umuganda

The last Saturday of every month, the country stops and works on something communal. Visitors are welcome to join.

Photo Bullet Honore / Pexels

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

The Congo Nile Trail

227 kilometres along Lake Kivu on foot or by bike, through coffee terraces and fishing villages.

Photo Edouard MIHIGO / Pexels

Bisoke Crater Lake

Three hours up through nettles and bamboo to a crater lake at 3,711 m, and three hours back down through mud.

Photo kevin yung / Pexels

Lodges Below the Volcanoes

A handful of very good lodges within sight of the park boundary, built so the trek starts after breakfast.

Photo Keegan Checks / Pexels
