ERITREA · Tourism

Asmara on the Plateau

A modernist capital on a highland plateau that UNESCO listed whole, a steam railway down to the Red Sea, and three hundred islands off Massawa that almost nobody visits.

East AfricaRegion
27Tourism experiences
Art deco Asmara, and the Dahlak islandsKnown for
01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Escarpment

The plateau drops two thousand metres to the Red Sea in about ninety kilometres.

Emba Soira

3,018 metres in the southern highlands, with terraced farming right up the sides.

Seasonal Only

The highland streams run in the summer rains and are dry the rest of the year.

The Red Sea Coast

No permanent rivers to speak of — the water here is the sea, and it is very warm.

The Dahlak Archipelago

Three hundred and fifty islands, four of them inhabited, and reefs almost nobody has dived.

The Green Belt

Semenawi Bahri, a strip of cloud forest on the escarpment an hour from the capital.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Danakil and Dahlak

Wild ass in the Danakil, dugong and turtle in the Dahlak archipelago.

Not the Business Here

There is no safari circuit. The wildlife is coastal and marine, and it is worth the swap.

Terraces That Hold the Soil

Highland terracing built by hand over centuries, and still the reason the topsoil is there.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Nine Groups, Two Faiths

Tigrinya, Tigre, Afar, Bilen and five more, roughly half Christian and half Muslim.

The Afar and the Rashaida

The Afar on the southern coast, and the Rashaida who came across from Arabia in the 1840s.

Injera, Zigni and Macchiato

Eritrean stews on injera, and an Italian coffee culture that never left.

Meskel and Eid

Meskel bonfires in September, and Eid in Massawa, in the same country and often the same street.

Filigree and Basketry

Silver filigree from Asmara's goldsmiths, and coiled straw baskets from the highlands.

Adulis and Qohaito

A Red Sea port that traded with Rome, and a highland city on the escarpment above it.

Asmara, Listed Whole

The first modernist city anywhere to be inscribed as a World Heritage site, in 2017.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Asmara and Massawa

One a cool art-deco plateau town, the other a coral-block Ottoman port on the sea.

Futurist and Coral

Fiat Tagliero's concrete wings in Asmara, and Massawa's buildings cut from the reef itself.

The Passeggiata

Asmara walks Harnet Avenue in the evening. Everybody does, and nobody hurries.

The Safest Capital Around

Asmara is small, walkable and calm, and children are everywhere in it after dark.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

The Massawa Road

Down the escarpment by road or by the restored steam railway, two thousand metres in a morning.

Cycling

Eritrea is a cycling country — the Tour of Eritrea climbs the escarpment.

Modest, and Improving

A handful of restored Italian-era hotels in Asmara. Nothing on the coast yet.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Deco in Low Light

Asmara's facades in the late afternoon, when the plaster goes warm and the streets empty.

The Tank Graveyard

A hillside outside Asmara stacked with the wreckage of thirty years of war.
