MOROCCO · Tourism

Where the Atlas Meets the Sahara

Four thousand metres of High Atlas within three hours of the Atlantic, four imperial cities, and the edge of the Sahara at Merzouga — a country you can cross from surf to dunes in two days.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Draa and the Dades

Palm oases strung along the Draa, and gorges cut red through the Dades and the Todra.

The High Atlas

Toubkal is 4,167 metres and the highest point in North Africa, walkable in two days from Imlil.

Ouzoud

A hundred metres of water in three stages, with macaques in the olive trees beside it.

The Draa River

The longest river in Morocco, mostly dry, and green the whole way where it is not.

The Atlantic Coast

Essaouira's wind, Taghazout's point breaks, and the long empty beaches between them.

The Cedar Forest

The Middle Atlas cedar, some of it a thousand years old, above Ifrane and Azrou.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Barbary Macaques and the Argan Forest

Macaques in the cedar forest above Azrou, and goats standing in the argan trees near Essaouira.

Desert Country

Not the safari of the east — the wildlife here is desert-adapted, and you look for it at first light.

Oasis Agriculture

The khettara irrigation channels still working, and the argan cooperatives run by women.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Amazigh and Arab

Three Amazigh languages, Arabic, French and Spanish, and a culture that predates all of them.

The People of the Atlas

Village life at two thousand metres, where the year is still organised around the snow.

Tagine, Bread and Mint

Slow-cooked tagine, bread baked in the neighbourhood oven, and tea poured from height.

Moussem and Gnaoua

The Gnaoua festival in Essaouira in June, and the moussems that mark the harvest.

Zellige, Leather and Wool

Tile cut by hand in Fez, tanned leather, and carpets woven to patterns held in memory.

Volubilis and the Kasbahs

A Roman city on the plain at Volubilis, and Ait Benhaddou standing above the Ounila.

The Imperial Cities

Fez, Marrakesh, Meknes and Rabat — four capitals, each of a different dynasty.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Marrakesh and Casablanca

Jemaa el-Fnaa at dusk, and the Atlantic modernism of Casablanca an hour up the road.

Riad and Medina

Rooms built inward around a courtyard, because the street was never meant to see in.

The Souk in the Morning

Before the tour groups, the medina is simply where people buy their vegetables.

Easy Travelling

Short drives, good roads, and a train line down the coast that children enjoy.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Dunes, Gorges and Surf

Trekking in the Atlas, surfing the Atlantic points, and a night out on the erg at Chegaga.

Trek, Surf, Ride

Multi-day Atlas treks, Atlantic surf schools, and horses on the Essaouira sand.

Desert Camps and Riads

Erg Chigaga camps well beyond the day-trip dunes, and riads restored room by room.

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Light on Earth Walls

Pisé takes late light the way nothing else does, which is why every photographer comes here.

Chefchaouen and the Rif

The blue town in the Rif, and a mountain range most itineraries drive straight past.
