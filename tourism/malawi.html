MALAWI · Tourism

The Lake

Lake Malawi holds more freshwater fish species than any lake on earth, Liwonde and Majete have been restocked to the point of holding all the big five, and the Mulanje massif rises three thousand metres straight off the plain.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Rift and the Massif

The Rift Valley floor with the lake in it, and Mulanje standing three thousand metres above the tea.

Mulanje

3,002 metres at Sapitwa, a granite massif with huts, paths and a week of walking on it.

Manchewe

Off the Nyika escarpment near Livingstonia, with a cave behind the water.

The Shire

The lake's only outlet, running south through Liwonde to the Zambezi.

Freshwater Beaches

Landlocked, and it does not matter: Cape Maclear, Nkhata Bay and Likoma have sand and clear water.

Nyika and Zomba

Montane grassland on Nyika at two thousand metres, and cedar forest on the Zomba plateau.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Cichlids, and the Big Five

Hundreds of endemic cichlids in the lake, and the big five back in the parks on shore.

Liwonde and Majete

Both restocked from near-empty — Majete had nothing in 2003 and has the big five now.

Majete's Recovery

African Parks took on an empty park in 2003 and it has the big five and no poaching now.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

The Warm Heart

The reputation for friendliness is real, and it is the first thing every visitor mentions.

Chewa, Yao, Tumbuka, Lomwe

The Chewa Gule Wamkulu masquerade is on the UNESCO list and is danced still.

Nsima and Chambo

Nsima with relish, and chambo — a lake cichlid — grilled whole on the shore.

The Lake of Stars

A music festival on the shore, and Gule Wamkulu in the villages all year.

Carving and Chitenje

Blackwood carving, basketry, and the printed chitenje cloth worn everywhere.

Livingstonia

A mission on the escarpment built in 1894, with the manse, the church and the view intact.

Lake Malawi National Park

The first freshwater national park in the world, listed for its cichlids.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Lilongwe and Blantyre

A quiet capital, and an older commercial city with the country's first church in it.

Mission Stone and Lakeshore Brick

Scottish mission churches in stone, and fired brick along the lake.

The Beach at Cape Maclear

Fishermen, boats, children and visitors on the same strip of sand all day.

Easy, and Warm

Short distances, no malaria-free claim but a very manageable country with children.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Diving, Kayak and the Massif

Freshwater diving on the cichlid reefs, kayaking the lake shore, and Mulanje for a week.

Mulanje for a Week

Hut to hut across the massif, with porters, and paths that have been there a century.

Small and Well Run

Lakeshore lodges, Mkulumadzi at Majete, and Kaya Mawa on Likoma island.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Clear Water and Granite

The lake in September, when the water is at its clearest and the boulders go down twenty metres.

Likoma Island

A Malawian island inside Mozambican water, with a cathedral the size of Winchester's.
