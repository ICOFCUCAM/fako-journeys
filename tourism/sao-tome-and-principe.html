01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Obo

Rainforest covering a third of Sao Tome, from the peaks down to the shore.

Pico de Sao Tome

2,024 metres, in cloud most of the year, and a two-day walk to the top.

Sao Nicolau

A waterfall in the forest above Trindade, reached on a short walk from the road.

Rivers to the Sea

Short, steep rivers off the volcanic interior, and pools in most of them.

Praia Jale and Banana Beach

Turtle-nesting sand in the south of Sao Tome, and the curved bay on Principe.

The Cocoa Forest

Plantations abandoned in the 1970s and taken back by the forest, with the trees still fruiting.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Endemic Birds

Twenty-eight species live on these islands and nowhere else — one of the highest rates on earth.

Sea, Not Savanna

There are no large mammals. What there is instead is birds, turtles and whales.

Cocoa, Grown and Made

Cooperatives grow it, and the chocolate is made on the island rather than shipped raw.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Creole, and Portuguese

Forro, Angolar and Principense creoles, and a culture built on the plantations and against them.

The Angolares

Descended from a shipwreck or a maroon community, depending on who tells it, and fishermen either way.

Calulu and Chocolate

Calulu — fish stewed with okra and palm oil — and chocolate made where the beans grow.

Auto de Floripes

A Principe street play that runs a whole day in August, performed since the sixteenth century.

Cocoa, Coffee and Carving

Single-origin chocolate, island coffee, and carving in the plantation villages.

The Rocas

Plantation estates with hospitals, chapels and rail lines, most of them now half-ruined and lived in.

Principe Biosphere

The whole of Principe is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, and it is seven thousand people.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Sao Tome Town

A small Portuguese colonial capital with a fort, a market and a cathedral.

Portuguese Colonial and Roca

Pastel colonial in town, and the plantation architecture of the rocas in the interior.

The Fish Landing

Dugouts come in through the surf and the fish is sold on the sand within the hour.

Small and Easy

Short distances, calm bays and no dangerous animals — one of the easier African countries with children.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Walking and Diving

Cao Grande and the Pico on foot, and volcanic reef straight off the beaches.

Climbing the Pico

Two days through cloud forest, with a night in a shelter and a lot of rain.

Good, and Small

Restored rocas turned into hotels, and a serious eco-resort on Principe.
