01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Zambezi Valley

Escarpment, floodplain and winterthorn forest, with the river braiding through the middle of all of it.

The Nyika Plateau

Two thousand metres of rolling montane grassland on the Malawi border, with roan antelope and no heat.

Victoria and Kalambo

Victoria Falls needs no introduction; Kalambo, on the Tanzanian border, drops 235 metres in a single uninterrupted fall.

Zambezi, Kafue, Tanganyika

Three great river systems and the southern tip of Lake Tanganyika, the second deepest lake on earth.

Lake Tanganyika Shore

Clear freshwater, sand and forested hills at Nsumbu, about as far from anywhere as Zambia gets.

Miombo and Winterthorn

Miombo woodland turning red at the start of the rains, and the winterthorn groves the elephants feed under.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Leopard Country

The South Luangwa has one of the densest leopard populations in Africa, and the best chance of seeing one hunting.

The Walking Safari

Invented in the Luangwa in the 1950s: no vehicle, an armed scout, and everything at eye level.

Kafue and the Carnivores

Kafue is the size of Wales; the conservation programmes there fund anti-poaching across the whole Greater Kafue system.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Lozi, Bemba, Tonga

Seventy-three ethnic groups, seven official languages, and a royal barge ceremony older than the country.

The Barotse Floodplain

The Lozi live with the flood — moving cattle, homes and the king himself when the Zambezi rises.

Nshima and Kapenta

Maize nshima with relish, dried kapenta from the lakes, and fresh bream out of the Zambezi.

Kuomboka

When the floodplain fills, the Litunga is paddled to high ground on the Nalikwanda barge, drummed the whole way.

Basketry and Copper

Tight coiled baskets from the Western Province, and copper worked on the belt that built the economy.

Livingstone

The old colonial capital above the falls, its museum, and the tree where the man himself is said to have camped.

Mosi-oa-Tunya, UNESCO

World Heritage since 1989, shared with Zimbabwe, and named by the Kololo long before anyone renamed it.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Lusaka

The centre of everything: Kabwata market, the Great East Road, and the flights that get you to the parks.

Livingstone's Verandas

Corrugated iron and deep colonial verandas along Mosi-oa-Tunya Road, built for heat and never modernised.

Markets and the Great North Road

Roadside charcoal and tomatoes, long-haul trucks to the copperbelt, and a bus that leaves when it fills.

Village Around the Park

Community campsites and craft cooperatives on the Luangwa boundary, funded by the same beds that fill the lodges.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Devil's Pool and the Gorge

Swim to the lip of Victoria Falls in low water, then raft the grade five rapids in the Batoka gorge below it.

Canoeing the Lower Zambezi

Three days downstream past hippo pods and elephant crossings, camping on islands, no engine at any point.

Bush Camps, Six Beds

Seasonal bush camps in the Luangwa that take six guests, close at the rains, and are walked between.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Luangwa at Last Light

The river bends, the hippo surface and the dust goes gold about forty minutes before the drive has to end.

Liuwa Plain

The second largest wildebeest migration in Africa, on a plain almost nobody has heard of, reachable only in the dry.
