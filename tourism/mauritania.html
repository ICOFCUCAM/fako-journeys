The Iron Ore Train

A two-kilometre freight train across the Sahara you can ride on top of, four caravan towns with medieval manuscript libraries still in family hands, and the largest bird colony in West Africa at the Banc d'Arguin.

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Adrar

A sandstone plateau cut by palm-filled canyons, with the old caravan towns in them.

Photo Moaz Tobok / Pexels

The Kediet ej Jill

915 metres of magnetite near Zouerat, so iron-rich it deflects a compass.

Photo m0us3b .1 / Pexels

Guelta de Matmata

Permanent pools in the rock south of Atar, with crocodiles that have been there since the Sahara was wet.

Photo Frederick Adegoke Snr. / Pexels

The Senegal River

The southern border, and the only permanent river in the country.

Photo Ben Khatry / Pexels

Seven Hundred Kilometres of Atlantic

Cold current, big surf, and the Imraguen fishing villages working with dolphins.

Photo Shivansh Sharma / Pexels

The Palmeraies

Date palm groves in the Adrar canyons — the only shade for hundreds of kilometres.

Photo Engin Akyurt / Pexels

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

The Banc d'Arguin

Two million wading birds winter here, on the largest tidal flats in West Africa.

Photo Francesco Ungaro / Pexels

Desert, Not Savanna

The wildlife is birds and desert-adapted mammals, and the coast is where it concentrates.

Photo Kent Zhong / Pexels

Two Million Birds

The Banc d'Arguin is a Ramsar site and one of the most important wintering grounds on earth.

Photo Juanita Theron / Pexels

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Moor and Fulani

Bidhan and Haratin Moors across the north, Fulani, Soninke and Wolof along the river.

Photo Faruk Tokluoğlu / Pexels

The Imraguen

A coastal people who fish with lanchas and, by tradition, with the help of dolphins.

Photo Kazys Photography / Pexels

Thieboudienne and Three Teas

Fish and rice from the river south, and the three-glass tea ceremony everywhere.

Photo Cup of Couple / Pexels

The Chinguetti Festival

Poetry, camel racing and manuscript readings in the old caravan town.

Photo Talha Riaz / Pexels

Silver, Leather and Chests

Moorish silver, tooled leather cushions, and the painted wooden chests of the Adrar.

Photo Mark Henry Rood / Pexels

Chinguetti and Ouadane

Twelfth-century caravan towns in dry stone, half-buried by the dunes now.

Photo Hale Ş / Pexels

The Manuscript Libraries

Family libraries in Chinguetti holding astronomy, law and poetry from the middle ages.

Photo Tahir Xəlfəquliyev / Pexels

Cities & everyday life

Nouakchott and Nouadhibou

A capital built from nothing in 1958, and a port with the world's biggest ship graveyard.

Photo Ben Khatry / Pexels

Dry Stone and Sand

Ksour built of flat stone without mortar, and streets that the dunes are taking back.

Photo mg shotz / Pexels

The Fish Market at Sunset

The pirogues come in at Nouakchott beach and the whole catch is sold on the sand.

Photo El moustapha Ivekou / Pexels

Big Country for Small Children

Distances are long and facilities are few. Better for teenagers than for toddlers.

Photo Moaz Tobok / Pexels
