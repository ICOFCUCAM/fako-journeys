A World in One Country

Table Mountain over Cape Town, Big Five in the Kruger lowveld, the Drakensberg escarpment, two oceans meeting, and eleven official languages.

A world in one countryKnown for
01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Fynbos and the Garden Route

The Cape Floral Kingdom is the smallest and richest of the six on earth, and most of it grows nowhere else.

The Drakensberg

The Amphitheatre wall runs five kilometres and drops a kilometre, with San rock art in the caves beneath it.

Tugela, Off the Amphitheatre

Nearly a thousand metres in five leaps off the Drakensberg escarpment — among the highest falls on earth.

Blyde River Canyon

The third largest canyon in the world and the greenest, with the Three Rondavels standing in the middle of it.

Two Oceans

Cold Atlantic on one side of the Cape peninsula, warm Indian on the other, forty minutes apart.

Tsitsikamma

Old yellowwood forest running down to a rocky coast, with the Storms River mouth cut through the middle.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

The Big Five, Close

Kruger and the private Sabi Sands reserves beside it, where the leopards have been watched for generations.

Sabi Sands, Off-Road

Private traverse means the vehicle can leave the road, which is why the sightings are what they are.

Marine and Rhino Work

Whale watching at Hermanus, penguins at Boulders, and rhino monitoring in the reserves that fund it.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Eleven Languages

Zulu, Xhosa, Afrikaans and eight more, plus the Cape Malay cooking that came with the slave trade.

Zulu, Xhosa, Ndebele

Ndebele wall painting, Zulu beadwork with its own grammar, and Xhosa initiation still practised in the Eastern Cape.

Braai, Bunny Chow, Curry

Fire is a national institution; Durban curry and Cape Malay bobotie came from two very different arrivals.

Carnival and AfrikaBurn

The Cape Town Carnival in March, the Kaapse Klopse minstrels in January, and AfrikaBurn out in the Karoo.

Ndebele Beadwork

Beadwork, wire sculpture and telephone-wire baskets, sold everywhere and made properly in very few places.

Robben Island

Twelve kilometres off Cape Town, a political prison for three decades, now guided by the men who were held there.

The Cradle of Humankind

The Sterkfontein caves have produced more early hominin fossils than any other site on the planet.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Cape Town and Johannesburg

One built around a mountain and a harbour, the other on a gold reef, and nothing about them is alike.

Bo-Kaap and Cape Dutch

Painted Bo-Kaap terraces on the slope of Signal Hill, and gabled Cape Dutch homesteads out in the winelands.

Soweto, Sunday

Vilakazi Street, the shisa nyama grills, and a township of a million people that runs at its own pace.

Township Tourism, Done Right

Guided by residents, spending in the neighbourhood, and honest about what apartheid planning actually built.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Bloukrans and the Cage

The highest commercial bridge bungee in the world, and great white cage diving out of Gansbaai.

Lion's Head at Dawn

Ninety minutes up a spiral path with chains near the top, for the whole peninsula at sunrise.

The Winelands

Franschhoek and Stellenbosch: Cape Dutch estates, serious kitchens, and a wine tram that means nobody drives.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Chapman's Peak Drive

Nine kilometres cut into the cliff between Hout Bay and Noordhoek, and every turnout is a photograph.

The Wild Coast and Karoo

Hole in the Wall and the Transkei green hills, and the Karoo's enormous silence in the other direction.
