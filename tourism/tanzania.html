TANZANIA · Tourism

Into the Wild

The Serengeti and the Ngorongoro crater, the highest mountain in Africa, and a spice island with a thousand-year-old stone town on it.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Ngorongoro Crater

A collapsed caldera twenty kilometres across with its own permanent population of thirty thousand animals.

Kilimanjaro, 5,895 m

The highest free-standing mountain on earth: rainforest, moorland, alpine desert and glacier in five days of walking.

Materuni, Under Kilimanjaro

Eighty metres of cold water through Chagga coffee farms on the lower slopes, an easy walk from Moshi.

Manyara and the Rufiji

Flamingo and tree-climbing lions at Manyara; the Rufiji braiding through Nyerere, the largest park in Africa.

Zanzibar, Nungwi to Paje

White sand, warm shallow water and dhows still built by hand on the beach at Nungwi.

Udzungwa and Jozani

The Udzungwa scarp holds species found nowhere else; Jozani holds the red colobus, also found nowhere else.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

The Big Five, and Then Some

Lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhino, plus the largest elephant herds left in East Africa.

Following the Migration

Calving on the southern plains in February, the Grumeti in June, the Mara river in August — the circuit never stops.

Ngorongoro, Shared Ground

The conservation area is the rare model where Maasai pastoralists and wildlife legally use the same land.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Maasai, Chagga, Swahili

Cattle-keepers on the northern plains, coffee farmers on the mountain, and a coast that has always faced east.

The Hadzabe of Lake Eyasi

One of the last hunter-gatherer peoples in Africa, speaking a click language unrelated to anything nearby.

Spice, Ugali, Urojo

Cloves and cardamom from the Zanzibar plantations, grilled seafood at Forodhani, and ugali everywhere else.

Sauti za Busara

Four days of live African music inside the old Arab fort in Stone Town, every February.

Tingatinga and Makonde

Enamel-bright Tingatinga painting from Dar es Salaam, and Makonde ebony carving from the southern plateau.

Stone Town and Kilwa

Stone Town is UNESCO-listed; Kilwa Kisiwani was the richest port on the Swahili coast six centuries ago.

Olduvai and the Coast

Olduvai Gorge holds the earliest human record; the coast holds a thousand years of Indian Ocean trade.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Dar es Salaam

Harbour of peace: the ferry terminal, the fish market at Kivukoni, and the dhows still working the channel.

The Doors of Stone Town

Brass-studded Indian doors and carved Omani frames, on coral-rag houses in alleys too narrow to drive.

Dala Dalas and Dhows

Minibuses inland, dhows on the water, and a fish auction at Kivukoni that starts before it is light.

Boma and Coffee Farm

Maasai boma visits on the crater rim road, and Chagga coffee walks on the lower slopes of Kilimanjaro.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Uhuru Peak by Machame

Six days up the Machame route, one very long night to the summit, and a sunrise you have to earn.

Snorkelling Mnemba

The atoll off the northeast tip of Zanzibar, plus kite surfing at Paje and diving at Pemba.

Camps and Spice Islands

Mobile camps that follow the migration, and beach houses on the Zanzibar east coast with nothing either side.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Acacia, Kopje, Sunset

The Serengeti kopjes at the end of the day, and the migration column stretching past the edge of the frame.

Ruaha and Katavi

The south and west: huge elephant populations, hippo crowded into shrinking pools, and no other vehicles.
