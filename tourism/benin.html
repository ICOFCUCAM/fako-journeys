BENIN · Tourism

Ganvie

Ouidah is the source of vodun and a departure point of the Atlantic slave trade, Abomey holds the earthen palaces of the kings of Dahomey, and Ganvie is a town of thirty thousand people on stilts in a lagoon.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Pendjari and the Atakora

The Atakora hills in the northwest, and the Pendjari river running along the park.

The Atakora Range

Six hundred metres of quartzite ridge in the northwest, with the Somba country under it.

Tanougou

Falls off the Atakora into a deep pool, swimmable and cold.

Lake Nokoue and the Oueme

The lagoon behind Cotonou, and the Oueme running down to it through the delta.

A Hundred Kilometres of Atlantic

Grand-Popo and Ouidah, with heavy surf and coconut palms right down to the sand.

The Sacred Forests

Kpasse at Ouidah and the groves around it, protected because they are vodun sites.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Pendjari

Lion, elephant, buffalo and cheetah in one of the last strongholds of the West African lion.

The W-Arly-Pendjari Complex

The largest intact ecosystem in West Africa, run across three countries.

Pendjari, Community-Run

Managed since 2017 by African Parks with the villages around it, and the lion count is up.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Where Vodun Comes From

Not a folk survival — a living religion with millions of followers, and a public holiday.

Fon, Yoruba, Bariba, Somba

The Fon of Dahomey in the south, the Somba in the Atakora with their fortified houses.

Amiwo and Grilled Fish

Amiwo — maize with tomato and chicken — and fish straight off the Grand-Popo beach.

The Vodun Festival

10 January at Ouidah, on the beach, and it is a religious event before it is a spectacle.

Applique and Bronze

The Abomey applique banners that recorded the kings' reigns, and Fon bronze casting.

The Royal Palaces of Abomey

Twelve palaces of the kings of Dahomey, in earth, with bas-reliefs telling the history.

The Route of the Slaves

Four kilometres from Ouidah's auction square to the Door of No Return on the beach.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Cotonou, Porto-Novo, Abomey

A port that never stops, a colonial capital, and the seat of a kingdom.

Earth and Afro-Brazilian

Somba tata towers in the north, and Afro-Brazilian merchant houses in Ouidah and Porto-Novo.

Zemidjan

The motorcycle taxis are the transport system, and their yellow shirts are the uniform.

Ganvie by Pirogue

A whole town on water, reached by boat, which no child has ever found boring.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Pirogue and Pendjari

Poling through Ganvie in the morning, and a game drive in the north two days later.

Walking the Atakora

Somba villages, quartzite ridges and the Tanougou pool, on foot from Natitingou.

A Few Good Ones

Lodges at Pendjari and on the coast at Grand-Popo, and good hotels in Cotonou.

Stilts and Reflection

Ganvie at first light, when the water is still and the houses double.

Possotome and Lake Aheme

A quiet lake west of Ouidah with fishing villages and hot springs on its shore.
