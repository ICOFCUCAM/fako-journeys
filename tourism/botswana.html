BOTSWANA · Tourism

A Delta That Never Reaches the Sea

The Okavango empties into the Kalahari and evaporates, and the whole country is organised around that fact — low-volume, high-value safari, mokoro channels, and the salt pans of Makgadikgadi.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Delta, Pan and Desert

Water in the north, salt pans in the middle, and Kalahari sand for the rest of it.

Tsodilo

No real mountains — Tsodilo's four hills hold four thousand rock paintings instead.

No Falls, and the Zambezi Next Door

The water here spreads rather than drops; Victoria Falls is ninety minutes from Kasane.

The Okavango and the Chobe

A mokoro poled through papyrus channels is the correct speed for this country.

Landlocked

The nearest sea is a thousand kilometres away, and nobody here seems to mind.

Riverine and Mopane

Riverine forest along the Chobe, and mopane woodland across the north.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

The Largest Elephant Population

Chobe holds more elephant than anywhere on earth, and they come to the river at four.

Low Volume, High Value

Fewer beds, larger concessions, and game drives that do not meet another vehicle.

Community Trusts

Concessions leased through community trusts, so the villages hold the lease and the income.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Batswana and San

Setswana across the country, and San communities in the Kalahari with the oldest culture on earth.

The Kalahari San

Tracking with San guides, who will find in an hour what nobody else sees at all.

Seswaa and Bogobe

Slow-pounded beef and sorghum porridge, and braai wherever there is a fire.

Maitisong and Dithubaruba

The Maitisong festival in Gaborone, and the Kweneng cultural festival at Dithubaruba.

Basketry

Palm baskets woven in the delta panhandle, dyed with bark, and collected worldwide.

Tsodilo Hills

Four thousand paintings on four hills the San call the resting place of the gods.

Diamonds and Democracy

Independent since 1966, and the one African country that used its diamonds well.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Maun and Kasane

Not really cities — two safari towns with airstrips, which is what the country needs.

Tent, Deck and Sand

Camps built on decks above the flood, taken down when the concession lease ends.

Cattle Posts

Away from the parks the country is cattle, and the cattle post is the centre of it.

Not the Cheapest, but Quiet

Not a budget destination — but the children will not be sharing a sighting with ten vehicles.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Mokoro, Walking, Horseback

Poled canoes, walking safaris on the islands, and some of the best riding safaris anywhere.

Mokoro and Walking

Poling, walking and riding — this is the country where you get out of the vehicle.

The Concession Camps

Private concessions the size of small countries, with six tents on them.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Water and Light

Aerial work over the delta, and reflections that only exist for the weeks of the flood.

The Makgadikgadi

Drive onto the pan at night and there is no horizon and no sound at all.
