Two Thousand Kilometres of Coast

The Indian Ocean coast of southern Africa — the Bazaruto and Quirimbas archipelagos, Ilha de Moçambique, Gorongosa's restored wildlife, and a Portuguese-Swahili culture that exists nowhere else.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Reef and Miombo

Coral reef the whole coast, miombo woodland inland, and the Rift's southern end at Gorongosa.

Mount Namuli and Gorongosa

Namuli at 2,419 metres, and Gorongosa's own mountain with rainforest on top of it.

Murchison of the South

Waterfalls off the Zambezi escarpment, and the Lugenda's rapids in the far north.

The Zambezi and Cahora Bassa

The Zambezi's last five hundred kilometres, and the Cahora Bassa lake above them.

Bazaruto and the Quirimbas

Dune islands in the south, coral islands in the north, and dhows between them.

Coastal Forest

The coastal dry forest of the north, and the mangrove that lines most of the delta.

Wildlife

Gorongosa

A park that lost almost everything in the war and has spent twenty years getting it back.

Niassa and Gorongosa

Niassa is one of the largest reserves in Africa, and among the least visited anywhere.

Gorongosa's Restoration

Local employment, local science, and a park brought back from nothing — worth understanding.

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Portuguese and Swahili

Five hundred years of Portuguese on a Swahili coast, and a culture made from both.

Makonde and Makua

Makonde carving in the north, and the Makua women's mussiro face paint.

Peri-Peri and Matapa

Prawns with peri-peri, and matapa — cassava leaf and coconut — with rice.

Marrabenta and Timbila

Marrabenta on the coast, and the Chopi timbila orchestras of Inhambane.

Carving and Basketry

Makonde blackwood carving, and the palm basketry of the northern islands.

Ilha de Moçambique

The old capital, on a coral island a kilometre long, with a fort at one end.

The Swahili Coast

Ibo, Angoche and Sofala — Indian Ocean trading towns older than the Portuguese.

Coral Stone and Colonial

Coral rag on the islands, and Portuguese modernism through Maputo and Beira.

The Beach Market

Fish landed and sold on the sand, and the dhow that brought it still on the beach.

Warm Water All Year

Shallow, warm and calm inside the reef — as easy a sea as the continent offers.
