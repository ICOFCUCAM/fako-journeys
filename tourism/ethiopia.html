ETHIOPIA · Tourism

The Roof of Africa

The only African country never colonised, with its own alphabet, calendar and church — rock-hewn churches at Lalibela, the Simien escarpment above four thousand metres, and the Danakil below sea level.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Escarpment and Rift

The Rift Valley cuts the whole country in half, with highlands either side of it.

The Simiens

Ras Dashen is 4,550 metres; the trek to it runs along the escarpment for days.

The Blue Nile Falls

Tis Issat — the water that smokes — where the Blue Nile leaves Lake Tana.

Lake Tana

The source of the Blue Nile, with monasteries on its islands still in use.

No Coast, and No Loss

Landlocked since 1993 — the water here is the Rift lakes and the crater lakes of Bishoftu.

Harenna and the Church Forests

Harenna's cloud forest in Bale, and the church forests that are the last woodland of the north.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Gelada and Walia Ibex

The gelada lives nowhere else on earth, and grazes the Simien escarpment in troops of hundreds.

Bale and the Ethiopian Wolf

The Bale mountains hold the last five hundred Ethiopian wolves, on the Sanetti plateau.

Community Conservation

Guides and scouts drawn from the villages inside the parks, which is why the wolves are still there.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Its Own Everything

Its own alphabet, its own calendar with thirteen months, and its own church since the fourth century.

Omo Valley

The lower Omo holds sixteen peoples in one valley, and the visits should be arranged carefully.

Injera and Doro Wat

Sourdough injera under everything, and fasting food that has made vegans of visitors.

Timkat and Meskel

Timkat in January at Gondar, and Meskel bonfires across the country in September.

Weaving and Silver

Handwoven shemma cotton, and Coptic crosses in silver, each region with its own shape.

Lalibela

Eleven churches cut down into solid rock in the twelfth century, still in use every day.

Aksum and Gondar

The Aksumite stelae, and the seventeenth-century castles of Gondar built by Fasilides.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Addis Ababa

Founded 1886, at 2,355 metres, and the diplomatic capital of the continent.

Round Houses and Rock Churches

The tukul, and the rock architecture of Tigray cut into cliff faces you have to climb.

Coffee, Properly

Coffee was found here, and it is still roasted, ground and poured in front of you.

Highland Travel

Cool highland air, short flights between the historic sites, and no malaria at altitude.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Trekking Country

Multi-day treks in the Simiens and Bale, and the Danakil at a hundred metres below sea level.

Trek and Climb

The Simien traverse, Bale's plateau, and the Tigray churches that need a rope.

Lodges on the Escarpment

Limalimo and Bale Mountain Lodge — small, sited well, and built by people who stayed.

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Faces and Escarpments

The light at four thousand metres, and portraiture that has to be asked for properly.

Tigray

Rock churches in cliff faces reached by hand and foot, with almost nobody else there.
