The Tenere

The Tenere is the emptiest desert on earth, the Air massif rises out of it to two thousand metres, and the last West African giraffes live wild an hour from the capital.

West AfricaRegion
27Tourism experiences
The Air, the Tenere, and the last giraffes of West AfricaKnown for
01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Air Massif

Granite mountains standing two thousand metres out of the sand, with guelta pools in them.

Photo SERHAT TUĞ / Pexels

Mont Idoukal-n-Taghes

2,022 metres in the Air, and the high point of a range full of rock art.

Photo Jeffry Surianto / Pexels

Guelta Pools

Permanent rock pools in the Air, the only standing water for hundreds of kilometres.

Photo Isaac Naph / Pexels

The Niger at Niamey

The river runs 550 kilometres across the southwest and the whole country leans on it.

Photo Sagar Thapa / Pexels

Sandbars on the River

Landlocked, and the swimming is off the sandbanks at Niamey when the river is low.

Photo Abdulrahman Abubakar / Pexels

The Doum Palm Valleys

Palm-filled wadis in the Air, green lines through absolutely bare rock.

Photo Engin Akyurt / Pexels

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

The Last West African Giraffe

About six hundred of them at Kouré, the only wild population left in the whole of West Africa.

Photo Regan Dsouza / Pexels

W National Park

Shared with Benin and Burkina Faso — lion, elephant and buffalo on the Niger river.

Photo Camille Cox / Pexels

Giraffes Beside Farms

Kouré's giraffes live among fields, and the community is paid to keep it that way.

Photo alameen .ng / Pexels

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Tuareg, Hausa, Zarma, Fulani

Tuareg and Toubou in the north, Hausa and Zarma in the south, Wodaabe in between.

Photo Sani Maikatanga / Pexels

The Wodaabe

The Gerewol, where the men dress and dance and the women choose.

Photo Sani Maikatanga / Pexels

Millet, Djerma Rice and Kilishi

Millet paste with sauce, and kilishi — beef dried thin and spiced, the best in the region.

Photo Tito Zzzz / Pexels

The Cure Salee

Tuareg and Wodaabe gather at In-Gall in September when the salt pastures come up.

Photo Ila Bappa Ibrahim / Pexels

Tuareg Silver and Leather

The Agadez cross in silver, and camel saddles and boxes in tooled leather.

Photo Sani Maikatanga / Pexels

Agadez

A mud minaret twenty-seven metres high, and the Sultanate that has stood behind it since 1449.

Photo Sani Maikatanga / Pexels

Three Sites

Agadez, the Air and Tenere reserves, and W National Park — desert, mountain and river.

Photo Drinu Cutajar / Pexels

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Niamey and Agadez

A river capital with the best museum in the Sahel, and a caravan city at the desert edge.

Photo Marcia Salido / Pexels

Mud and Beams

Sahelian mud with projecting timber, and the Agadez minaret as its tallest expression.

Photo Sani Maikatanga / Pexels

The Niamey Grand Marche

Everything from millet to motorcycle parts, and the noise that goes with it.

Photo Ghufran Visuals / Pexels

Giraffes an Hour Away

Kouré is a morning trip from Niamey, on foot, and children can do it.

Photo alameen .ng / Pexels

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Crossing the Tenere

The Tree of Tenere marked the crossing until a truck hit it in 1973. The route is still run.

Photo 光曦 刘 / Pexels

The Air on Foot and Camel

Weeks of walking or riding between guelta and village, with Tuareg guides.

Photo mohamed aouni / Pexels

Very Little

A few hotels in Niamey. Everything north is camps and hospitality.

Photo Abhishek Navlakha / Pexels

Travel inspiration

Dunes and Nothing

The Tenere at either end of the day, where the only vertical thing is your own shadow.

Photo Luis Quintero / Pexels

The Bilma Salt Caravans

The azalai still runs from Agadez to Bilma and back, a thousand kilometres by camel.
