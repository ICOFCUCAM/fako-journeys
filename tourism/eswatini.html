Small, and Full

A kingdom you can cross in two hours, with rhino at Hlane and Mkhaya, granite whalebacks at Sibebe and Execution Rock, and the Umhlanga and Incwala ceremonies that are the living centre of the country's year.

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Highveld to Lowveld

Fourteen hundred metres in the west down to two hundred in the east, in sixty kilometres.

Sibebe

The largest exposed granite dome in the world after Uluru, and you walk up it.

Photo Alfo Medeiros / Pexels

Mantenga

A wide falls beside the cultural village in the Ezulwini valley.

Photo Mark Napier / Pexels

The Great Usutu

The country's main river, running east through a gorge to the Mozambican border.

Photo Kevyn Costa / Pexels

Landlocked, and Close

No coast — but the Mozambican beaches at Ponta do Ouro are two hours from Manzini.

Photo quang vinh / Pexels

Malolotja and Ngwempisi

Montane grassland and Afromontane forest in the northwest, with proper wilderness walking.

Photo Lucas Pezeta / Pexels

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Rhino at Mkhaya

Black and white rhino, tracked on foot, in a private reserve that has never lost one to poaching.

Photo Twilight Kenya / Pexels

Hlane and Mkhaya

Lion and elephant at Hlane, rhino and sable at Mkhaya, both small and both excellent.

Photo Theo Topolevsky / Pexels

Big Game Parks

One family has run Hlane, Mkhaya and Mlilwane since 1960, and brought the rhino back.

Photo Chané Bruwer / Pexels

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

A Living Monarchy

Umhlanga and Incwala are not performances — they are the constitutional year.

Photo Josiah Matthew / Pexels

The Swazi

One nation, one language, and a set of ceremonies that everyone in the country takes part in.

Photo Khaya Motsa / Pexels

Sidvudvu and Emasi

Pumpkin with maize, emasi soured milk, and braai everywhere.

Photo KATRIN BOLOVTSOVA / Pexels

Umhlanga and Incwala

The reed dance in late August, and Incwala, the kingship ceremony, in December and January.

Photo Asso Myron / Pexels

Candles, Glass and Weaving

Swazi Candles, Ngwenya Glass from recycled bottles, and Gone Rural's grass weaving.

Photo Magda Ehlers / Pexels

Ngwenya Mine

Iron ore mined here forty-three thousand years ago — among the oldest mines on earth.

Photo BonusBrownie Photography / Pexels

Nothing Listed, Everything Living

No World Heritage sites, and a ceremonial calendar that has never been interrupted.

Photo Kawê Rodrigues / Pexels
