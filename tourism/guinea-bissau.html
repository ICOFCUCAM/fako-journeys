GUINEA-BISSAU · Tourism

The Bijagos

An archipelago of eighty-eight islands with saltwater hippos, nesting turtles and a matrilineal society that still chooses its own kings — and a mainland of mangrove and rice paddy behind it.

West AfricaRegion
27Tourism experiences
The Bijagos, and eighty-eight islandsKnown for
01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Mangrove and Tide

The tidal range here is five metres, and the channels between the islands change with it.

Photo Thilina Alagiyawanna / Pexels

Flat Country

The highest point is three hundred metres. This is a country of water, not relief.

Photo NGUYỄN THÀNH NHƠN / Pexels

None to Speak Of

No falls — the land has nowhere to fall from. The water here moves sideways with the tide.

Photo Vincent Eshun / Pexels

The Geba and the Corubal

Two rivers cutting deep estuaries into the mainland, navigable a long way up.

Photo Pok Rie / Pexels

Island Sand

Bubaque, Rubane and Orango have empty palm-backed beaches and warm water.

Photo Denys Gromov / Pexels

Palm and Sacred Grove

Oil palm through the islands, and groves that are ceremonial ground and off limits.

Photo Azinuddin Bin Abas / Pexels

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Saltwater Hippos

The hippos of Orango swim in the sea between islands, which almost no others do.

Photo K / Pexels

Turtles at Poilao

One of the largest green turtle nesting sites in the Atlantic, on an uninhabited island.

Photo Nitin Yadav / Pexels

Community Conservation

The island communities police the turtle beaches themselves, and it works.

Photo Mikhail Nilov / Pexels

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Matrilineal, and Still So

Bijago women choose husbands, own the houses, and hold the religious authority.

Photo Sani Maikatanga / Pexels

The Bijagos

Age grades govern life, and the vaca-bruto initiation is danced in a bull mask.

Photo Abduljalil Attahir / Pexels

Rice, Cashew and Bica

Rice from the paddies, cashew as the whole export economy, and Portuguese coffee.

Photo Jay Gleaton / Pexels

Carnival in Bissau

Masks, drums and neighbourhood competition, Portuguese in form and entirely local in content.

Photo Alex Fanaso / Pexels

Carving and Cloth

Bijago bull and shark masks, and the woven pano de pente cloth used as currency.

Photo David Ramsamy / Pexels

Cacheu

A slaving fort of 1588 on the river, and one of the oldest European buildings in West Africa.

Photo MAG Photography / Pexels

The Bijagos Biosphere

The whole archipelago is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, run with the island communities.

Photo Isaac Naph / Pexels

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Bissau

A small Portuguese-built capital with a decaying Bissau Velho quarter and very good music.

Photo Guillaume Kremer / Pexels

Portuguese Colonial and Palm Thatch

Tiled colonial fronts in Bissau, and rectangular palm-thatch houses through the islands.

Photo Vincent Rivaud / Pexels

Sunday at the Beach

Bissau goes to Bubaque and the near islands at weekends, by ferry, with a stereo.

Photo that_photographer / Pexels

Boats and Beaches

Not for very small children — the boats are open and the tides are serious.

Photo Prince Enos / Pexels

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Island Hopping by Pirogue

There are no bridges. Everything is boats, tides and waiting, and that is the pleasure of it.

Photo Vietnam Hidden Light / Pexels

Fishing the Channels

Barracuda and tarpon in the passes between islands, on light tackle.

Photo Ben Khatry / Pexels

Two or Three Lodges

Small fishing and eco-lodges on Rubane and Orango, and not much else anywhere.

Photo Ana Kenk / Pexels
