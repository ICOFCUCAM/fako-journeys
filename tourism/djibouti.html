Lake Assal

Lake Assal at 155 metres below sea level, whale sharks in the Gulf of Tadjoura every winter, and the Ardoukoba rift where the continent is actively splitting — all of it in a country the size of Wales.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Afar Triangle

Three tectonic plates pulling apart, and you can stand in the crack.

The Goda and Mabla

Mousa Ali at 2,028 metres, and juniper forest above the coastal plain at Day.

Nothing Permanent

There are no rivers here. The water is in wadis, springs and the sea.

Lake Abbe

Limestone chimneys steaming on a dry lake bed, on the Ethiopian border.

The Gulf of Tadjoura

Beaches at Sable Blanc and the Sept Freres islands, and reef straight off the sand.

The Last Juniper

The Day forest is a relic of a wetter Horn, and it is shrinking.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Whale Sharks

They come into the Gulf of Tadjoura from November, in numbers, close to shore.

Day Forest

Beira antelope and the last Djibouti francolin in the juniper of the Goda mountains.

Salt, Cut by Hand

Slabs still cut from Assal and loaded onto camels, on a route a thousand years old.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Afar and Somali

Two peoples, French and Arabic official, and a port that half the Horn depends on.

The Afar

Salt caravans still come up from Assal, and the Afar have worked that trade for centuries.

Skoudehkaris and Yemeni Fish

Spiced rice with lamb, and whole fish baked in a Yemeni clay oven on the coast.

Independence Day

27 June, and the Afar and Somali dance traditions that come out for it.

Weaving and Woodwork

Afar mats and milk vessels, and carved wooden headrests that double as stools.

Ottoman Tadjoura

One of the oldest towns on this coast, with seven mosques and a sultan's history.

The Lake Abbe Landscape

Not listed, but the chimneys are on every list of the strangest places on earth.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Djibouti City

A French-Arab port city with a covered market, and the Horn's busiest container terminal.

Arcades and Coral

Colonial arcades in the European quarter, and coral-block houses in the old town.

Khat at Four

The flight lands from Ethiopia and the city stops for it. Every afternoon.

Sable Blanc

Shallow, warm, empty white sand an hour and a half from the capital.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Snorkelling and Rift Walking

Swimming with whale sharks in the morning, walking a spreading rift in the afternoon.

The Ardoukoba

A volcano that erupted in 1978 as the rift opened. The lava field is still bare.

Small and Getting Better

A few good hotels in the capital and a diving lodge on the gulf.

06

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

White on Turquoise

Lake Assal at midday, when the salt is blinding and the water is the wrong blue.

The Sept Freres

Seven islands at the mouth of the gulf, with the best reef in the country round them.
