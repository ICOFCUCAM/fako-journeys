ALGERIA · Tourism

The Sahara Begins Here

Four-fifths Sahara, a Mediterranean coast of Roman cities, and the Hoggar and Tassili massifs in the deep south — rock art nine thousand years old on a plateau the size of Britain.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Hoggar and Tassili

Volcanic spires in the Hoggar, and a sandstone plateau at Tassili n'Ajjer eroded into streets.

The Djurdjura and the Hoggar

Snow on the Djurdjura above Kabylie in winter; Mount Tahat at 2,908 metres in the far south.

Water in a Dry Country

The falls at Kefrida in Bejaia, in a green fold of the coast that surprises everyone.

Chotts and Oases

Salt lakes that mirror the sky at Chott Melrhir, and the palmeraie at Timimoun.

A Thousand Kilometres of Mediterranean

The Turquoise Coast west of Algiers, and the coves at Jijel under limestone cliffs.

The Kabylie Cedars

Atlas cedar on the Djurdjura ridge, some of it four hundred years old.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

What Lives in the Sand

Fennec fox, addax and the last Saharan cheetah, all of them nocturnal and all of them rare.

Desert Watching, Not Game Driving

There is no safari circuit here. You go out at dawn with somebody who reads tracks.

The Case for Going Now

Algeria takes very few visitors for its size, and everything about it is the better for that.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Three Languages Before Lunch

Darija, Tamazight and French, and a coastline that has been argued over for three thousand years.

The Kabyles and the Tuareg

Berber-speaking Kabylie in the north, and the Tuareg of the Hoggar in the far south.

Couscous on Friday

Chorba, m'hadjeb, and the Friday couscous that stops the whole country.

Yennayer and the Sufi Moussems

The Amazigh new year in January, and the moussems that fill desert towns for a week.

Silver, Wool and Pottery

Kabyle silver with coral and enamel, Beni Isguen weaving, and the pottery of Maatkas.

Timgad and Djemila

Two Roman cities the desert kept: Timgad's grid is complete, Djemila's forum still stands.

Seven Sites, Nine Thousand Years

Tassili's rock art records a Sahara with rivers in it, and the M'zab valley is still lived in.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Algiers, White on the Bay

The Casbah stacked above the harbour, and the boulevards the French cut below it.

The Casbah and the M'zab

Ottoman houses on top of each other in Algiers, and five fortified towns in the M'zab built to one plan.

Coffee and Argument

The cafe is the institution. So is the argument in it.

Beaches and Roman Stones

Tipaza has a Roman city on a beach, which solves most family arguments at once.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Crossing the Tassili

A week on foot with Tuareg guides among the sandstone towers, sleeping out.

Walking the North

The Chrea forest above Blida, the Gouraya cliffs at Bejaia, and the Djurdjura ridge.

Rare, and Getting Less So

Restored Ottoman houses in the Casbah, and desert camps in the Hoggar run to a standard.

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Light on Sandstone

Tassili at either end of the day, when the rock goes from ochre to red to purple.

Ghardaia

Five towns in a valley, built by the Mozabites in the eleventh century and still run their way.
