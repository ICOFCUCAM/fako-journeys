The Fouta Djallon

The Niger, the Senegal and the Gambia all rise in the Fouta Djallon, a sandstone highland of waterfalls and cliffs — and Conakry has produced more of the region's music than anywhere except Bamako.

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Where the Rivers Rise

The Niger, the Senegal and the Gambia all begin within a hundred kilometres of each other.

Mount Nimba

1,752 metres on the Liberian border, with species that live on that mountain and nowhere else.

Ditinn and Kinkon

Ditinn drops a hundred metres off a sheer cliff; Kinkon runs through a gorge below Pita.

The Upper Niger

It rises near the Sierra Leone border and is already wide by the time it leaves the country.

Three Hundred Kilometres of Atlantic

Mangrove coast with the Iles de Los off Conakry, and beaches on Roume and Kassa.

The Guinean Forest

Ziama and Diecke in the southeast — some of the last high forest in West Africa.

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Chimpanzees at Bossou

One of the best-studied wild chimpanzee groups anywhere, and one that uses stone tools.

The Forest, Not the Plain

There is no savanna circuit. What Guinea has is forest primates and the Nimba massif.

Nimba's Endemics

The mountain has species found on it and nowhere else, and an iron deposit under them.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Where the Music Came From

Bembeya Jazz, the Ballets Africains, and a state that funded orchestras for two decades.

Fulani, Malinke, Susu

Fulani in the Fouta, Malinke in the upper Niger country, Susu on the coast.

Riz Gras and Fouti

Rice with palm oil and fish, and the sour milk and maize of the Fouta.

Masked Dance in the Forest Region

The forest peoples of the southeast keep mask societies that are not for visitors, and some that are.

Weaving and Indigo

Fouta indigo cloth, Malinke weaving, and the leatherwork of the highland towns.

The Almamy's Fouta

The Fouta Djallon was a theocratic Fulani state for a century and a half before the French came.

Nimba, Listed

Mount Nimba is a World Heritage site for its endemic toad, viviparous, that lives on the ridge.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Conakry

A capital on a peninsula, with the Grande Mosquee and the sea on both sides.

Colonial and Fouta Vernacular

French colonial in Conakry, and round thatched houses with painted walls in the highlands.

Market Day in Labe

The highland market fills with Fulani from every village within a day's walk.

Waterfalls You Can Swim In

Most of the Fouta's falls have a pool at the bottom and a path down to it.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Hiking the Fouta

Village to village between waterfalls and cliffs, with the best walking network in the region.

Trekking the Highlands

The Fouta has marked routes, village guides and huts, which is rare in the region.

Modest

Hotels in Conakry and a few in Labe and Dalaba. The Fouta runs on homestays.

Cliffs and Water

The Fouta escarpments in the hour after the rain stops, when everything is running.

The Dame de Mali

A rock face near Mali-ville shaped like a woman's profile, at the top of the Fouta.
