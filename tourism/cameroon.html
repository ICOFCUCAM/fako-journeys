01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Six Climates, One Country

The Cameroon Volcanic Line runs from the Gulf of Guinea to the Mandara mountains, changing the landscape every two hours of driving.

Photo Ariel Nathan ADA MBITA / Unsplash

The 4,095 m Volcano

Mount Cameroon is the highest point in West Africa and still active — three days up through farm, forest, grass and bare cinder.

Photo Christian Aime Nanga / Unsplash

Where the River Meets the Sea

The Lobé falls near Kribi drop straight over black rock into the Atlantic — one of very few waterfalls on earth that do.

Photo Renato Ponte / Pexels

The Benoue and the Crater Lakes

Gallery forest along the Bénoué in the north, and the volcanic crater lakes of Barombi Mbo and Nyos in the west.

Photo Joseph Siewe / Unsplash

Black Sand and Warm Water

Volcanic grey-black sand at Limbe, white sand and fishing pirogues at Kribi, warm all year on both.

Photo George Njukeng / Pexels

Inside Korup

One of the oldest rainforests in Africa, with a canopy suspension bridge over the Mana river and 400 tree species.

Photo Hervé Kashama / Pexels

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Wildlife of the Rainforest

Forest elephants, drills and chimpanzees in Korup and Dja — one of the oldest and least disturbed rainforests in Africa.

Photo Angelo Casto / Unsplash

Sahel Safari at Waza

Flat acacia savanna in the Far North, where elephant, giraffe and antelope gather at the last dry-season waterholes.

Photo Angelo Casto / Unsplash

Korup, Dja and the Village Fund

Ecotourism permits in Korup and Dja are issued through the villages, and the mountain trek runs on the Mount CEO rotation.

Photo Edouard TAMBA / Unsplash

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

The Grassfields Kingdoms

Bamileke and Bamoun chiefdoms in the west, still functioning, with palaces, secret societies and their own calendars.

Photo Edouard TAMBA / Unsplash

Fulani, Mafa and Bamileke

Two hundred and fifty ethnic groups and as many languages, from the Mafa terraces of the Mandara to the Fulani of the Sahel.

Photo Abduljalil Attahir / Pexels

Ndole, Poulet DG and Grilled Fish

Bitterleaf and groundnut ndolé, plantain, and whole fish grilled on the sand at Kribi within sight of the boat.

Photo Ariel Nathan ADA MBITA / Unsplash

Nguon at Foumban

The Bamoun Nguon, a thousand years old and UNESCO-listed, where the sultan answers to his people before the dancing starts.

Photo Vincent Lockhart / Unsplash

The Foumban Bronze Casters

Lost-wax bronze, beaded thrones and carved masks made on the craft street the same way they were three centuries ago.

Photo Ariel Nathan ADA MBITA / Unsplash

The Sultan's Palace

The 1917 palace at Foumban and its museum of the Bamoun kings, plus the German colonial buildings still standing in Buea.

Photo K / Pexels

Dja, Rhumsiki and the Mandara

The Dja Faunal Reserve is UNESCO World Heritage; the volcanic spires above Rhumsiki are the image on the old banknote.

Photo Anchau / Pexels

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Douala and Yaounde

The port that moves the country and the hill capital that governs it, ninety minutes apart by air and nothing alike.

Photo K / Pexels

Chefferie and Colonial Buea

Bamileke chefferie compounds with carved posts and conical roofs, and the German Schloss on the hill above Buea.

Photo Dokun Ayano / Pexels

Marche Central, Motos, Rain

Bilingual, loud and unbothered: Douala's markets, the moto-taxis, and the sudden rain everyone waits out under an awning.

Photo Edouard TAMBA / Unsplash

Village and Mountain Together

Guides and porters come from the mountain villages in rotation, and the trail money goes back into the village fund.

Photo Joseph Siewe / Unsplash

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Summit, Crater, Coast

The Guinness route to 4,095 m, down the north flank to Mann's Spring, and out to the sea on the same trip.

Photo Thasura Kumarasinghe / Pexels

The Ring Road on Foot

Crater lakes, tea slopes and the Ring Road through the Bamenda highlands, walkable in sections between 1,200 and 2,000 m.

Photo Balázs Gábor / Pexels

Kribi, Slowly

Small beach hotels south of Kribi, a driver who knows the road, and nothing at all scheduled after lunch.

Photo David Iloba / Pexels

Travel inspiration

Cloud on the Rainforest

Wet season cloud sitting on the forest above Buea, and dry season light on the Mandara spires — two countries, one lens.

The 1999 Lava Fields

Black lava tongues from the 1999 and 2000 flows running down to the sea near Bakingili, with green already coming back.
