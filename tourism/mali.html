MALI · Tourism

The Great Mosque of Djenne

The mud-brick mosque at Djenne is the largest building of its kind on earth, the Bandiagara escarpment holds Dogon villages built into the cliff, and the Niger loops nine hundred kilometres north into the desert before turning back.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Niger Bend

The river runs north into the Sahara, thinks better of it, and turns southeast.

The Bandiagara Escarpment

A hundred and fifty kilometres of sandstone cliff, with villages built into the face of it.

Falls at Woroni

Small falls on the sandstone plateau above Bandiagara, running only after the rains.

The Niger and Lake Debo

Pinasses working the river between Mopti and Timbuktu, and the inland delta spreading behind them.

River Sand, Not Sea

Landlocked — the beaches here are sandbars on the Niger, and people swim off them.

The Gallery Forest

Ribbons of woodland along the river, and the baobabs standing alone on the plain behind.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Elephants in the Gourma

The Gourma herd makes the longest elephant migration in Africa, on a loop into Burkina Faso.

Not the Business Here

There is no lodge circuit. The Gourma elephants are a conservation story, not a game drive.

Re-Plastering Djenne

Every year the town rebuilds the mosque's surface by hand in a single day.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Where the Blues Came From

Ali Farka Toure, Toumani Diabate, Salif Keita and Tinariwen are all from here.

Dogon, Bambara, Tuareg, Fulani

Dogon on the escarpment, Bambara on the river, Tuareg and Fulani on the plains north of it.

Tigadege Na and Capitaine

Groundnut stew, and Nile perch grilled whole on the riverbank at Mopti.

The Sigui and the Dama

Dogon masked ceremonies, the Sigui every sixty years and the Dama for the dead.

Bogolan and Bamana Sculpture

Mud-dyed bogolanfini cloth, Bamana chiwara antelope crests, and Dogon carved doors.

Timbuktu

Three mud mosques, and a library tradition that ran to hundreds of thousands of manuscripts.

Four Sites, All at Risk

Djenne, Timbuktu, Bandiagara and the Tomb of Askia — every one of them on the danger list.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Bamako and Mopti

A capital sprawling both sides of the Niger, and a port town built on three islands.

Sudano-Sahelian

Mud walls, projecting palm beams, and buttressed towers — a style that belongs to this river.

Monday Market at Djenne

The whole delta comes to the square in front of the mosque, once a week.

Hard Country for It

The security situation makes most of Mali unvisitable at present, and that is the honest answer.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Walking the Dogon Country

Village to village along the escarpment, sleeping on roofs, four days or fourteen.

The Escarpment on Foot

The Dogon country is walking country, and always has been.

Almost None

A handful of hotels in Bamako. The rest of the country is guesthouses and roofs.

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Mud and Long Shadows

Djenne an hour before sunset, when the toron beams throw stripes down the wall.

The Tomb of Askia

A pyramid of mud at Gao, built in 1495 for the emperor of Songhai.
