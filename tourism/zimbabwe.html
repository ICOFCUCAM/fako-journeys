Great Zimbabwe and the Zambezi

Victoria Falls on one border, Great Zimbabwe's stone city in the interior, Hwange's elephant herds and Mana Pools — the park where the walking safari was refined into an art.

Great Zimbabwe and the ZambeziKnown for
01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Highveld and Escarpment

Granite kopjes across the highveld, and the Zambezi escarpment dropping into the valley.

The Eastern Highlands

Nyanga, Vumba and Chimanimani along the Mozambique border, up to 2,592 metres.

Victoria Falls

Mosi-oa-Tunya — a mile and a half wide, and a hundred metres down, in full flood.

The Zambezi and Kariba

The Zambezi above the falls, and Kariba's drowned forest downstream of them.

Landlocked

No coast — the water here is the Zambezi, Kariba, and the pools of the Mana floodplain.

Msasa and Miombo

Miombo woodland across the country, and msasa that comes into red leaf in September.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Hwange's Elephant

Forty thousand elephant in Hwange, drinking at pumped pans through the dry season.

Mana Pools on Foot

The park where you may walk unguided, and where the elephant stand on their hind legs.

CAMPFIRE

The community wildlife programme that much of the region's conservation policy was built on.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Shona and Ndebele

Shona across the centre and east, Ndebele in the west, and stone sculpture from both.

The Stone Sculptors

Zimbabwean stone sculpture is the country's clearest contemporary export.

Sadza and Braai

Sadza with relish, and meat over coals wherever there is a fire and company.

HIFA and the Harvest

The Harare arts festival, and the harvest ceremonies that still open the season.

Stone, Basket and Wire

Serpentine carving, Binga basketry, and wire sculpture sold on every roadside.

Great Zimbabwe

Eight hundred years old, mortarless, and the largest stone structure south of the Sahara.

Khami and Matobo

Khami's terraces, and the Matobo hills where Mzilikazi and Rhodes are both buried.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Harare and Bulawayo

Harare on the highveld, and Bulawayo's wide colonial grid built for turning an ox wagon.

Dry Stone

Great Zimbabwe's coursed granite is the architecture, and nothing since has matched it.

Kombis and Musika

The country runs on kombis and market days, both of which are worth an afternoon.

Falls and Elephant

Victoria Falls, a steam train, and elephant at a waterhole — a good week for children.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Walking, Canoeing, Rafting

Canoe the Zambezi past hippo, raft the gorge below the falls, and walk Mana with a guide.

Raft, Canoe, Walk

Grade five rafting below the falls, multi-day canoe trails, and walking safaris in Mana.

The Old Safari Standard

Small owner-run camps in Mana and Hwange, run by guides with the country's toughest licence.

Chimanimani

A mountain range on the Mozambique border that you walk into and camp in.
