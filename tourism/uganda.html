01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Green, End to End

Crater lakes around Fort Portal, papyrus swamp on the Nile, and the Rwenzori snowline sitting on the equator.

Photo Ivan Sabayuki / Unsplash

The Mountains of the Moon

The Rwenzori range holds glaciers three degrees from the equator, and Margherita Peak at 5,109 m.

Photo Mwesigwa Joel / Pexels

Murchison, Through the Gap

The entire Victoria Nile forced through a seven-metre gap and dropped forty-three metres into the gorge below.

Photo K3N / Unsplash

Where the Nile Begins

The Nile leaves Lake Victoria at Jinja; Lake Bunyonyi holds twenty-nine islands and no bilharzia.

Photo Ana Kenk / Pexels

The Ssese Islands

Freshwater beaches and fishing villages on Lake Victoria, an hour and a half by ferry from Entebbe.

Kibale and Bwindi

Thirteen primate species in Kibale, and in Bwindi a forest so old and so thick it was never logged.

Photo Dmitrii Zhodzishskii / Unsplash

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Wildlife Encounters

Mountain gorillas in Bwindi, chimpanzees in Kibale, and tree-climbing lions in the Ishasha sector.

Photo Gabriel Schumacher / Unsplash

Queen Elizabeth and Kidepo

Kob on the Kasenyi plains, elephant on the Kazinga Channel, and Kidepo Valley with almost nobody in it.

Photo Dmitrii Zhodzishskii / Unsplash

The Gorilla Permit

Permit revenue funds the park and pays a share to the parishes around it — which is why the gorilla numbers are rising.

Photo Gabriel Schumacher / Unsplash

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

The Kingdom of Buganda

Uganda's kingdoms were never abolished for long — Buganda, Bunyoro and Toro still hold court.

Batwa and Karamojong

The Batwa, forest people of the southwest, and the Karamojong cattle-keepers of the northeast plains.

Rolex, Matoke, Luwombo

Steamed green banana with groundnut sauce, chicken cooked in banana leaf, and an egg rolled in a chapati on every corner.

Photo saidi musiitwa / Pexels

Imbalu and Nyege Nyege

The Bagisu Imbalu season in the east, and four days of music on the banks of the Nile at Jinja.

Photo Mwesigwa Joel / Pexels

Bark Cloth and Basketry

Bark cloth from the mutuba fig, made in Buganda for six hundred years and now UNESCO-listed.

Photo Gary Chapman / Unsplash

The Kasubi Tombs

The burial ground of four Buganda kings, thatched, enormous and rebuilt by the clans who have always maintained it.

Photo From Salih / Pexels

Kingdoms Still Standing

Royal drums, regalia and clan structures that survived the protectorate, the 1966 crisis and the 1970s.

Photo Random Institute / Unsplash

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Kampala on Seven Hills

A capital built across hills and traffic, with the Gaddafi Mosque minaret giving the best view of all of it.

Photo josh_ dago__ / Pexels

Minaret, Dome and Thatch

The Uganda National Mosque, the Namirembe and Rubaga cathedrals facing each other across two hills.

Photo josh_ dago__ / Pexels

Boda Bodas and Owino

The boda boda is the transport system; Owino market is where the whole city goes to buy everything.

Photo Enock Ojambo / Pexels

Villages at the Forest Edge

Community walks in Buhoma and Nkuringo, run by the villages that share the forest with the gorillas.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Grade Five on the Nile

White-water rafting below the source at Jinja, plus bungee, kayaking and the long walk up Sipi.

Photo b40deep / Unsplash

Sipi, Elgon, Bisoke

Three waterfalls and a coffee walk at Sipi, then the long crater rim of Mount Elgon above it.

Lodges at the Forest Edge

Small lodges on the Bwindi ridge and the Kazinga Channel, close enough to walk to the trailhead after breakfast.

Photo 2H Media / Unsplash

Travel inspiration

The frames people come home with.

Crater Lakes at First Light

The Ndali-Kasenda crater field before the haze lifts, and the Ishasha fig trees in the late afternoon.

Photo Mwesigwa Joel / Pexels

Kidepo Valley

The furthest park from Kampala and the emptiest: Narus valley, the Morungole range, and cheetah nobody photographs.

Photo Anthony Desrochers / Pexels
