MADAGASCAR · Tourism

An Island That Evolved Alone

Separated from Africa 160 million years ago, with ninety per cent of its wildlife found nowhere else — lemurs, baobabs, the spiny forest of the south, and rainforest along the eastern escarpment.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

Six Ecosystems on One Island

Rainforest east, highlands centre, spiny forest south, dry deciduous west, and reef all round.

The Highlands and Maromokotro

The central highlands run to two thousand metres; Maromokotro reaches 2,876 in the Tsaratanana.

The Eastern Escarpment

Short, steep rivers off the escarpment, and waterfalls through the rainforest at Andasibe.

The Tsiribihina

Three days downriver by pirogue through country with no road in it.

Nosy Be and the East Coast

Reef, whale sharks and humpbacks off Nosy Be, and long empty sand on the east coast.

Rainforest and Spiny Forest

Andasibe and Ranomafana in the east, and the spiny forest of the south which is like nowhere.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

A Hundred Kinds of Lemur

A hundred and seven species and subspecies of lemur, every one of them endemic.

Not a Safari Island

No lions and no elephant — the wildlife here is small, nocturnal, and worth the torchlight.

The Reason to Come Carefully

Deforestation is the country's crisis, and the parks that work are the ones the villages run.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Malagasy, and Eighteen Peoples

One language across the island, of Austronesian origin, and eighteen distinct peoples speaking it.

Famadihana and the Ancestors

The ancestors are present here in a way that shapes the calendar and the architecture.

Romazava and Rice

Rice three times a day, and romazava — beef and greens — with it.

Hira Gasy and Donia

Hira gasy troupes in the highlands, and the Donia festival on Nosy Be in June.

Silk, Raffia and Zafimaniry

Wild silk lambas, raffia work, and the Zafimaniry woodcarving of the south-east.

The Rova and the Royal Hills

Ambohimanga, the royal hill of the Merina kings above Antananarivo.

Pirates and Kingdoms

Île Sainte-Marie's pirate cemetery, and the Merina kingdom that unified the island.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Antananarivo

A capital built up twelve hills, with stairs instead of streets in half of it.

Highland Brick

Two-storey red brick with a wooden balcony, and nothing else in Africa looks like it.

The Zebu Market

Zebu cattle are wealth here, and the markets that trade them are the real calendar.

Slow, and Worth It

Roads are slow and distances are long — but the children will hold a chameleon.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

The RN7 and the Tsingy

The road south through the highlands, and the limestone tsingy you cross on via ferrata.

Trek, Paddle, Dive

Rainforest trekking, the Tsiribihina by pirogue, and reef diving off Nosy Be.

Small Lodges

Owner-run lodges at the park gates, and beach places on Nosy Be with a dozen rooms.

Travel inspiration

Baobabs at Six

The avenue at Morondava for twenty minutes at either end of the day, and nothing else like it.

Masoala

The largest protected rainforest in the country, reached by boat, with whales in the bay.
