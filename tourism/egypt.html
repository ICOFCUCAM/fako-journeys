Five Thousand Years

The oldest continuous tourism in the world, and still the reason most people first think of Africa — the Giza plateau, the Valley of the Kings, Abu Simbel, and the Red Sea reefs on the other coast.

01

Featured destinations

Where the country actually takes you.

The Nile and the Desert

A green ribbon a few kilometres wide, and sand for a thousand kilometres either side of it.

Sinai

Mount Sinai and Saint Catherine's, and granite peaks over two thousand metres above the Red Sea.

The First Cataract

The Nile's cataracts at Aswan — rapids rather than falls, and the reason the boats stopped here.

The Nile Itself

Feluccas under sail at Aswan, and a river that has been the country's main road for millennia.

The Red Sea

Some of the clearest reef diving on earth, an hour's flight from the pyramids.

The Fayoum

Not forest — the Fayoum's palm and olive, and the acacia of Wadi el-Rayan.

02

Wildlife & safari

What lives here, and how you see it without disturbing it.

Wildlife

Birds of the Delta

Two million migrating birds use the Delta and the Red Sea coast as a flyway each year.

The Western Desert

Not big game — desert fox, gazelle and the silence of the Great Sand Sea.

Reef and Oasis

Reef protection on the Red Sea, and oasis agriculture that has managed water for millennia.

03

Culture & heritage

The part of a country you cannot photograph from a vehicle.

Culture

Coptic, Islamic, Pharaonic

Three layers still in daily use, sometimes in the same street in old Cairo.

Nubians and Bedouin

Nubian villages above Aswan and Bedouin communities in the Sinai and the Western Desert.

Ful, Koshary and Bread

Breakfast is ful and bread; lunch is koshary; both are older than most cuisines.

Moulids and Ramadan

The moulid processions, and Cairo at iftar when the whole city eats at once.

Papyrus, Glass and Cotton

Hand-blown glass in Cairo, Egyptian cotton, and papyrus made the way it always was.

The Valley of the Kings

Sixty-odd tombs cut into a single dry valley, most of them painted floor to ceiling.

Karnak, Luxor, Abu Simbel

Karnak took two thousand years to build; Abu Simbel was moved sixty metres to save it.

04

Cities & everyday life

Where people actually live between the landmarks.

Cairo and Alexandria

Twenty million people on the Nile, and a Mediterranean city that remembers being Greek.

Mamluk Cairo

The mosques, sabils and madrasas of the medieval city, largely still standing.

The Corniche at Dusk

Families out along the river, tea on the pavement, and the traffic finally slowing.

Straightforward with Children

Short internal flights, boats instead of buses, and a story at every stop.

05

Adventure & outdoors

For travellers who would rather walk it than watch it.

Adventure

Desert Crossings

Diving the Red Sea, walking the Sinai with Bedouin guides, and crossing the sand sea by vehicle.

Dive, Sail, Ride

Reef diving at Marsa Alam, feluccas at Aswan, and camels in the Western Desert.

Dahabiyas and Desert Camps

Small sailing dahabiyas instead of the big cruisers, and camps in the White Desert.

The Light at Six

Desert light at either end of the day is the whole reason to be up early here.

Siwa

Eight hundred kilometres west, a Berber-speaking oasis with its own language and salt lakes.
