Equatorial Guinea

A volcanic island, and turtles on the black sand.

Bioko is a volcanic island with cloud forest, seven primate species and four nesting turtle species on its southern beaches; the mainland at Monte Alen holds gorillas and forest elephant.

Landscapes
  • The Gran CalderaA collapsed volcanic crater in the south of Bioko, forested, and reached only on foot.
  • Pico Basile3,011 metres, the island's volcano, with a road most of the way up it.
  • IladyiA 250-metre cascade off the caldera rim in the south of Bioko.
  • The Wele and the MuniThe Wele runs across the mainland; the Muni estuary marks the Gabonese border.
  • Black Sand and TurtlesFour species nest on Bioko's southern beaches — leatherback, green, olive ridley and hawksbill.
  • Cloud Forest and LowlandCloud forest above a thousand metres on Bioko, and Congo Basin forest on the mainland.
Wildlife
  • Seven Primates on One IslandBioko has seven species, several of them found on that island and nowhere else.
  • Monte AlenMainland forest with gorilla, chimpanzee and forest elephant, walked rather than driven.
  • The Bioko Biodiversity ProgrammeUniversity-run monitoring of the turtle beaches and primates since 1998.
  • Walking BiokoThe south of the island has no roads. Everything there is done on foot.
People and culture
  • Spanish, Fang and BubiThe only Spanish-speaking country in Africa, with Fang on the mainland and Bubi on Bioko.
  • The BubiBioko's first people, with their own language, and a history separate from the mainland.
  • The AbiraA Bubi purification ceremony, danced with drums and rattles.
  • Carving and BasketryFang carving on the mainland, and Bubi basketry and wooden bells on Bioko.
  • Pepe Soup and MalambaPepper soup with fish, and malamba — palm wine sweetened with cane.
  • Beach and ForestArena Blanca on Bioko is calm and shaded, and Pico Basile is drivable.
  • Sunday After MassMalabo empties into the streets after the cathedral, and stays there.
Places
  • Malabo and BataThe island capital in a drowned crater, and the mainland's largest city on the coast.
  • Spanish ColonialMalabo's cathedral, customs house and shuttered merchant houses, in a Gulf of Guinea version of Spain.
  • Colonial MalaboSanta Isabel was a Spanish port town, and the cathedral and customs house still stand.
  • Nothing Listed YetNo World Heritage sites, and a colonial architecture that has been very little studied.
Ways to travel
  • Trekking the CalderaThree days on foot from Ureca to the Gran Caldera, through forest, with no road at all.
  • Business Hotels, MostlyMalabo and Bata have good hotels built for the oil industry, and Bioko's south has none.
  • Black Sand and GreenUreca in the rain, which is most of the time — the wettest place in Africa.
  • AnnobonA volcanic island six hundred kilometres out, with a crater lake and four flights a month.
