Seychelles

Granite islands in the Indian Ocean.

The only granite islands in the middle of an ocean anywhere on earth — Mahé, Praslin and La Digue, the coco de mer in the Vallée de Mai, and Aldabra's giant tortoises a thousand kilometres out.

01Highlights

What Seychelles is known for.

Six of the twenty-seven, and the ones most people come for. The rest are below — and none of them is filler.

Nature & Landscapes

Forty-Three Granite Islands

Granite in the inner islands, coral atolls in the outer, and a thousand kilometres between.

Wildlife

Endemic Everywhere

The black parrot, the magpie robin, and giant tortoises that live free on Curieuse.

Mountains

Morne Seychellois

Nine hundred metres of forested granite above Victoria, with trails through the mist forest.

Beaches & Coast

Anse Source d'Argent

The most photographed beach in the world, and the granite is the reason why.

Forests & Rainforest

The Vallée de Mai

Coco de mer palms in a valley on Praslin, and the largest seed of any plant on earth.

Culture

Creole

African, French, Indian and Chinese, in one Creole language and one kitchen.

03Why go

The case for Seychelles.

Why Visit This Country

Nowhere Else Is Built Like This

Granite in mid-ocean, a palm that grows nowhere else, and tortoises older than the state.

Hidden Gems

La Digue by Bicycle

No real roads and few cars — an island best measured in bicycle hours.

Eco Tourism

Half the Country Is Protected

Forty-two per cent of the land is national park, and the marine parks now match it.

Heritage

Aldabra

A thousand kilometres out, with a hundred thousand giant tortoises and almost no people.
