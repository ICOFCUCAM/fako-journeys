Seychelles

Granite islands in the Indian Ocean.

The only granite islands in the middle of an ocean anywhere on earth — Mahé, Praslin and La Digue, the coco de mer in the Vallée de Mai, and Aldabra's giant tortoises a thousand kilometres out.

Landscapes
  • Forty-Three Granite IslandsGranite in the inner islands, coral atolls in the outer, and a thousand kilometres between.
  • Morne SeychelloisNine hundred metres of forested granite above Victoria, with trails through the mist forest.
  • Sauzier and the StreamsShort granite streams off the ridges, and the Sauzier falls in the west of Mahé.
  • No Rivers, All SeaThe water here is the Indian Ocean, and the lagoons inside the reef.
  • Anse Source d'ArgentThe most photographed beach in the world, and the granite is the reason why.
  • The Vallée de MaiCoco de mer palms in a valley on Praslin, and the largest seed of any plant on earth.
Wildlife
  • Endemic EverywhereThe black parrot, the magpie robin, and giant tortoises that live free on Curieuse.
  • Not a Safari, a ReefThe game here is under the water — reef fish, turtle, and whale shark off Mahé in season.
  • Half the Country Is ProtectedForty-two per cent of the land is national park, and the marine parks now match it.
  • Dive, Kayak, CycleGranite dive sites, kayaks in the lagoons, and La Digue where the bicycle is the transport.
People and culture
  • CreoleAfrican, French, Indian and Chinese, in one Creole language and one kitchen.
  • Island LifeA hundred thousand people across a hundred and fifteen islands, most on three of them.
  • Festival KreolFestival Kreol in October, and the sega and moutya danced through it.
  • Boatbuilding and WeavingPirogue building on La Digue, and coconut palm weaving still done by hand.
  • Fish, Coconut and ChilliGrilled fish, coconut curry, breadfruit, and a chilli sauce that is not a suggestion.
  • Shallow, Warm, SafeLagoons inside the reef, no dangerous surf on most beaches, and short boat rides.
  • The Fish MarketVictoria's market at eight in the morning is the most useful hour on the island.
Places
  • VictoriaOne of the smallest capitals in the world, with a clock tower and a market.
  • Creole HousesSteep roofs, wide verandas and louvres — built for heat before anyone had a choice.
  • The PlantationsL'Union Estate on La Digue, with its vanilla, copra mill and colonial house.
  • AldabraA thousand kilometres out, with a hundred thousand giant tortoises and almost no people.
Ways to travel
  • Dive, Sail, WalkDiving the granite drop-offs, sailing the inner islands, and walking Mahé's ridge trails.
  • The Private IslandsNorth Island, Frégate and Denis — private islands with a dozen villas on each.
  • Granite and TurquoiseGrey granite, white sand and three shades of blue — the reason every camera comes here.
  • La Digue by BicycleNo real roads and few cars — an island best measured in bicycle hours.
