Two volcanic islands and a lot of cocoa.

Obo rainforest running from the peaks to the sea, volcanic plugs standing out of the forest, abandoned cocoa plantations being taken back by it, and some of the best chocolate in the world made where the beans grow.

Landscapes
  • OboRainforest covering a third of Sao Tome, from the peaks down to the shore.
  • Pico de Sao Tome2,024 metres, in cloud most of the year, and a two-day walk to the top.
  • Sao NicolauA waterfall in the forest above Trindade, reached on a short walk from the road.
  • Rivers to the SeaShort, steep rivers off the volcanic interior, and pools in most of them.
  • Praia Jale and Banana BeachTurtle-nesting sand in the south of Sao Tome, and the curved bay on Principe.
  • The Cocoa ForestPlantations abandoned in the 1970s and taken back by the forest, with the trees still fruiting.
Wildlife
  • Endemic BirdsTwenty-eight species live on these islands and nowhere else — one of the highest rates on earth.
  • Sea, Not SavannaThere are no large mammals. What there is instead is birds, turtles and whales.
  • Cocoa, Grown and MadeCooperatives grow it, and the chocolate is made on the island rather than shipped raw.
  • Climbing the PicoTwo days through cloud forest, with a night in a shelter and a lot of rain.
People and culture
  • Creole, and PortugueseForro, Angolar and Principense creoles, and a culture built on the plantations and against them.
  • The AngolaresDescended from a shipwreck or a maroon community, depending on who tells it, and fishermen either way.
  • Auto de FloripesA Principe street play that runs a whole day in August, performed since the sixteenth century.
  • Cocoa, Coffee and CarvingSingle-origin chocolate, island coffee, and carving in the plantation villages.
  • Calulu and ChocolateCalulu — fish stewed with okra and palm oil — and chocolate made where the beans grow.
  • Small and EasyShort distances, calm bays and no dangerous animals — one of the easier African countries with children.
  • The Fish LandingDugouts come in through the surf and the fish is sold on the sand within the hour.
Places
  • Sao Tome TownA small Portuguese colonial capital with a fort, a market and a cathedral.
  • Portuguese Colonial and RocaPastel colonial in town, and the plantation architecture of the rocas in the interior.
  • The RocasPlantation estates with hospitals, chapels and rail lines, most of them now half-ruined and lived in.
  • Principe BiosphereThe whole of Principe is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, and it is seven thousand people.
Ways to travel
  • Walking and DivingCao Grande and the Pico on foot, and volcanic reef straight off the beaches.
  • Good, and SmallRestored rocas turned into hotels, and a serious eco-resort on Principe.
  • Volcano and CanopyCao Grande through cloud in the early morning, from the road below it.
  • Roca Agostinho NetoThe largest plantation on the island, ruined, lived in, and quite unlike anywhere else.
