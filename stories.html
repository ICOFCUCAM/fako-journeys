AlgeriaWhy Algeria

The Roman cities of Tunisia, the Sahara of Libya and the Atlas of Morocco, with almost nobody in them.

AlgeriaGhardaia

Five towns in a valley, built by the Mozabites in the eleventh century and still run their way.

AngolaWhy Angola

A waterfall to rival Victoria's, a desert coast, and a capital that surprises everyone.

AngolaTundavala

A gap in the escarpment where the ground drops a thousand metres, with no railing.

BeninWhy Benin

Vodun at its source, the palaces of Dahomey, and West Africa's best-run national park.

BeninPossotome and Lake Aheme

A quiet lake west of Ouidah with fishing villages and hot springs on its shore.

BotswanaFewer People Than Elephants

Two and a half million people, a hundred and thirty thousand elephant, and space for both.

BotswanaThe Makgadikgadi

Drive onto the pan at night and there is no horizon and no sound at all.

Burkina FasoWhy Burkina Faso

The masks, the music and the cinema, in a country that has never traded on scenery.

Burkina FasoTiebele

Kassena compounds painted in black, white and ochre geometry by the women of the household.

BurundiWhy Burundi

The Nile's furthest source, the royal drums, and a lake almost nobody visits.

BurundiRusizi Delta

Hippos, birds and the Congo across the water, twenty minutes from the capital.

Cabo VerdeWhy Cabo Verde

An active volcano with vineyards in it, ridge walking above the Atlantic, and the morna.

Cabo VerdeBrava

The smallest inhabited island, reached only by boat, with flowers and no airport worth the name.

CameroonEverything, Within One Border

Sea level to Sahel in nine hundred kilometres, French and English on the same road, and almost nobody else on it.

CameroonThe 1999 Lava Fields

Black lava tongues from the 1999 and 2000 flows running down to the sea near Bakingili, with green already coming back.

Central African RepublicWhy the Central African Republic

A hundred forest elephants in one clearing, watched from a platform, by almost no one.

Central African RepublicChimpanzees at Bai Hokou

A second habituated group, and the walk in is as good as the sighting.

ChadWhy Chad

A national park that came back from the dead, and a desert massif almost nobody has seen.

ChadThe Guelta d'Archei

Camels come down a gorge to drink, and desert crocodiles live in the pool below them.

ComorosWhy Comoros

An active volcano, a living fossil, and a Swahili stone town nobody visits.

ComorosThe Coelacanth

Caught off these islands in 1938, having been extinct for eighty million years.

CongoWhy Congo

The best western lowland gorilla tracking anywhere, in forest that has never been cut.

CongoLesio-Louna

A gorilla sanctuary on the Bateke plateau, two hours from Brazzaville.

Côte d'IvoireThe Engine of the Region

The largest economy in francophone West Africa, and the easiest of them to travel.

Côte d'IvoireMan

The western hills, with waterfalls, mask villages and a mountain nobody climbs.

DjiboutiWhy Djibouti

Whale sharks, the lowest point in Africa and a rift valley opening, in a country you cross in a day.

DjiboutiThe Sept Freres

Seven islands at the mouth of the gulf, with the best reef in the country round them.

DR CongoWhy DR Congo

A lava lake, mountain gorillas, glaciers on the equator, and the okapi.

DR CongoLake Kivu

Volcanic islands, terraced hills and calm water, between Goma and Bukavu.
