01Highlights

What Ethiopia is known for.

Six of the twenty-seven, and the ones most people come for. The rest are below — and none of them is filler.

Nature & Landscapes

Escarpment and Rift

The Rift Valley cuts the whole country in half, with highlands either side of it.

Wildlife

Gelada and Walia Ibex

The gelada lives nowhere else on earth, and grazes the Simien escarpment in troops of hundreds.

Mountains

The Simiens

Ras Dashen is 4,550 metres; the trek to it runs along the escarpment for days.

Beaches & Coast

No Coast, and No Loss

Landlocked since 1993 — the water here is the Rift lakes and the crater lakes of Bishoftu.

Forests & Rainforest

Harenna and the Church Forests

Harenna's cloud forest in Bale, and the church forests that are the last woodland of the north.

Culture

Its Own Everything

Its own alphabet, its own calendar with thirteen months, and its own church since the fourth century.

Landscapes
  • Escarpment and RiftThe Rift Valley cuts the whole country in half, with highlands either side of it.
  • The SimiensRas Dashen is 4,550 metres; the trek to it runs along the escarpment for days.
  • The Blue Nile FallsTis Issat — the water that smokes — where the Blue Nile leaves Lake Tana.
  • Lake TanaThe source of the Blue Nile, with monasteries on its islands still in use.
  • No Coast, and No LossLandlocked since 1993 — the water here is the Rift lakes and the crater lakes of Bishoftu.
  • Harenna and the Church ForestsHarenna's cloud forest in Bale, and the church forests that are the last woodland of the north.
Wildlife
  • Gelada and Walia IbexThe gelada lives nowhere else on earth, and grazes the Simien escarpment in troops of hundreds.
  • Bale and the Ethiopian WolfThe Bale mountains hold the last five hundred Ethiopian wolves, on the Sanetti plateau.
  • Community ConservationGuides and scouts drawn from the villages inside the parks, which is why the wolves are still there.
  • Trek and ClimbThe Simien traverse, Bale's plateau, and the Tigray churches that need a rope.
People and culture
  • Its Own EverythingIts own alphabet, its own calendar with thirteen months, and its own church since the fourth century.
  • Omo ValleyThe lower Omo holds sixteen peoples in one valley, and the visits should be arranged carefully.
  • Timkat and MeskelTimkat in January at Gondar, and Meskel bonfires across the country in September.
  • Weaving and SilverHandwoven shemma cotton, and Coptic crosses in silver, each region with its own shape.
  • Injera and Doro WatSourdough injera under everything, and fasting food that has made vegans of visitors.
  • Highland TravelCool highland air, short flights between the historic sites, and no malaria at altitude.
  • Coffee, ProperlyCoffee was found here, and it is still roasted, ground and poured in front of you.
Places
  • Addis AbabaFounded 1886, at 2,355 metres, and the diplomatic capital of the continent.
  • Round Houses and Rock ChurchesThe tukul, and the rock architecture of Tigray cut into cliff faces you have to climb.
  • LalibelaEleven churches cut down into solid rock in the twelfth century, still in use every day.
  • Aksum and GondarThe Aksumite stelae, and the seventeenth-century castles of Gondar built by Fasilides.
Ways to travel
  • Trekking CountryMulti-day treks in the Simiens and Bale, and the Danakil at a hundred metres below sea level.
  • Lodges on the EscarpmentLimalimo and Bale Mountain Lodge — small, sited well, and built by people who stayed.
  • Faces and EscarpmentsThe light at four thousand metres, and portraiture that has to be asked for properly.
  • TigrayRock churches in cliff faces reached by hand and foot, with almost nobody else there.
