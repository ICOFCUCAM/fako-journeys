Who you are dealing with

Three companies’ worth of responsibility run through this product, and you are entitled to know which one you are dealing with at any moment — on the surface where it matters, not inferable from a footer.

  • experience

    Afrinkong a trading name

    Shows you Africa, and helps you turn that into a journey.

  • commercial

    Wankong LLC the company

    The company you contract with, and the one anything you pay goes to. Afrinkong is its trading name.

  • operations

    The named ground operation named per country

    The people on the ground who actually run your days.

Money is always the company

Everything that moves money or entitlement is Wankong LLC. Not Afrinkong, which is a name. Not the operator, who is a supplier. Journeys are quoted, invoiced and settled in US dollars to Wankong LLC, and Travel Points — when a programme is ever able to issue any — are issued by that same company under written terms.

Nothing on this site takes a payment today, which is exactly why the rule is written now. A surface that carries a card field or a “pay” button without naming the payee on itself fails a check, and that check was verified by building such a page and watching it fail.

Every act, and who performs it

All 10 of them. 7 declare who is acting. 6 of those are the ones where your money, your entitlement or your trip is at stake — the rest is the operator’s own desk saying that it is the operator’s, which is a courtesy rather than an obligation.

Two of them have two answers

A booking is an agreement with one company and days run by another, and telling you only half of that is how “who do I call” becomes unanswerable. So where an act has a second party, both are named above rather than one.

Support splits the same way, and the split is the useful part: before you travel it is Afrinkong; while you are travelling it is the people who are with you.

A link is not a URL

The first guard on this boundary classified links by address, with a list of operator paths forbidden in certain places. That is wrong twice over. Cameroon is one of fifty-four countries and belongs in Explore; that a ground operation is based there is a different fact about a different layer. And an operator’s own desk is perfectly correct on a page where you are explicitly dealing with that operator.

So a link is judged by entity, context, position and action together — any one of them alone gives the wrong answer. The same address in the body of a page about an operation is fine; in the primary navigation, or as a primary button, it is a misdirection. What turns a misdirection into a handover is declaring it. Crossing a boundary is fine. Crossing it silently is not.
