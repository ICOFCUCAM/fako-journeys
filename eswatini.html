Small, and full of everything.

A kingdom you can cross in two hours, with rhino at Hlane and Mkhaya, granite whalebacks at Sibebe and Execution Rock, and the Umhlanga and Incwala ceremonies that are the living centre of the country's year.

What Eswatini is known for.

Nature & Landscapes

Highveld to Lowveld

Fourteen hundred metres in the west down to two hundred in the east, in sixty kilometres.

Wildlife

Rhino at Mkhaya

Black and white rhino, tracked on foot, in a private reserve that has never lost one to poaching.

Mountains

Sibebe

The largest exposed granite dome in the world after Uluru, and you walk up it.

Beaches & Coast

Landlocked, and Close

No coast — but the Mozambican beaches at Ponta do Ouro are two hours from Manzini.

Forests & Rainforest

Malolotja and Ngwempisi

A Living Monarchy

Landscapes
  • Highveld to LowveldFourteen hundred metres in the west down to two hundred in the east, in sixty kilometres.
  • SibebeThe largest exposed granite dome in the world after Uluru, and you walk up it.
  • MantengaA wide falls beside the cultural village in the Ezulwini valley.
  • The Great UsutuThe country's main river, running east through a gorge to the Mozambican border.
  • Landlocked, and CloseNo coast — but the Mozambican beaches at Ponta do Ouro are two hours from Manzini.
  • Malolotja and NgwempisiMontane grassland and Afromontane forest in the northwest, with proper wilderness walking.
Wildlife
  • Rhino at MkhayaBlack and white rhino, tracked on foot, in a private reserve that has never lost one to poaching.
  • Hlane and MkhayaLion and elephant at Hlane, rhino and sable at Mkhaya, both small and both excellent.
  • Big Game ParksOne family has run Hlane, Mkhaya and Mlilwane since 1960, and brought the rhino back.
  • Walking MalolotjaMulti-day wilderness trails across grassland and gorge, with huts and no roads.
People and culture
  • A Living MonarchyUmhlanga and Incwala are not performances — they are the constitutional year.
  • The SwaziOne nation, one language, and a set of ceremonies that everyone in the country takes part in.
  • Umhlanga and IncwalaThe reed dance in late August, and Incwala, the kingship ceremony, in December and January.
  • Candles, Glass and WeavingSwazi Candles, Ngwenya Glass from recycled bottles, and Gone Rural's grass weaving.
  • Sidvudvu and EmasiPumpkin with maize, emasi soured milk, and braai everywhere.
  • Easy and SafeShort distances, malaria-light, rhino you can walk to, and a very gentle pace.
  • The Manzini MarketProduce downstairs, handicraft upstairs, and the whole country passing through on a Thursday.
Places
  • Mbabane and ManziniA small capital in the hills, and the commercial town and market in the middle.
  • Beehive and ColonialWoven beehive huts in the homesteads, and small colonial towns in stone.
  • Ngwenya MineIron ore mined here forty-three thousand years ago — among the oldest mines on earth.
  • Nothing Listed, Everything LivingNo World Heritage sites, and a ceremonial calendar that has never been interrupted.
Ways to travel
  • Canopy Tours and WhitewaterThe Malolotja canopy tour, and rafting the Great Usutu when it is up.
  • Small and GoodMkhaya's Stone Camp, and lodges through the Ezulwini valley.
  • Granite and GrassSibebe in the late afternoon, when the whole dome goes orange.
  • Ngwempisi GorgeA wilderness gorge in the west with hanging bridges and no vehicles.
