Landscapes
  • Highveld and EscarpmentGranite kopjes across the highveld, and the Zambezi escarpment dropping into the valley.
  • The Eastern HighlandsNyanga, Vumba and Chimanimani along the Mozambique border, up to 2,592 metres.
  • Victoria FallsMosi-oa-Tunya — a mile and a half wide, and a hundred metres down, in full flood.
  • The Zambezi and KaribaThe Zambezi above the falls, and Kariba's drowned forest downstream of them.
  • LandlockedNo coast — the water here is the Zambezi, Kariba, and the pools of the Mana floodplain.
  • Msasa and MiomboMiombo woodland across the country, and msasa that comes into red leaf in September.
Wildlife
  • Hwange's ElephantForty thousand elephant in Hwange, drinking at pumped pans through the dry season.
  • Mana Pools on FootThe park where you may walk unguided, and where the elephant stand on their hind legs.
  • CAMPFIREThe community wildlife programme that much of the region's conservation policy was built on.
  • Raft, Canoe, WalkGrade five rafting below the falls, multi-day canoe trails, and walking safaris in Mana.
People and culture
  • Shona and NdebeleShona across the centre and east, Ndebele in the west, and stone sculpture from both.
  • The Stone SculptorsZimbabwean stone sculpture is the country's clearest contemporary export.
  • HIFA and the HarvestThe Harare arts festival, and the harvest ceremonies that still open the season.
  • Stone, Basket and WireSerpentine carving, Binga basketry, and wire sculpture sold on every roadside.
  • Sadza and BraaiSadza with relish, and meat over coals wherever there is a fire and company.
  • Falls and ElephantVictoria Falls, a steam train, and elephant at a waterhole — a good week for children.
  • Kombis and MusikaThe country runs on kombis and market days, both of which are worth an afternoon.
Places
  • Harare and BulawayoHarare on the highveld, and Bulawayo's wide colonial grid built for turning an ox wagon.
  • Dry StoneGreat Zimbabwe's coursed granite is the architecture, and nothing since has matched it.
  • Great ZimbabweEight hundred years old, mortarless, and the largest stone structure south of the Sahara.
  • Khami and MatoboKhami's terraces, and the Matobo hills where Mzilikazi and Rhodes are both buried.
Ways to travel
  • Walking, Canoeing, RaftingCanoe the Zambezi past hippo, raft the gorge below the falls, and walk Mana with a guide.
  • The Old Safari StandardSmall owner-run camps in Mana and Hwange, run by guides with the country's toughest licence.
  • Albida LightThe winterthorn cathedrals of Mana Pools, in dust and low sun, are photographed everywhere.
  • ChimanimaniA mountain range on the Mozambique border that you walk into and camp in.
