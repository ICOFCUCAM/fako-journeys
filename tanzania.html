Nature & Landscapes

The Ngorongoro Crater

A collapsed caldera twenty kilometres across with its own permanent population of thirty thousand animals.

Wildlife

The Big Five, and Then Some

Lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhino, plus the largest elephant herds left in East Africa.

Mountains

Kilimanjaro, 5,895 m

The highest free-standing mountain on earth: rainforest, moorland, alpine desert and glacier in five days of walking.

Beaches & Coast

Zanzibar, Nungwi to Paje

White sand, warm shallow water and dhows still built by hand on the beach at Nungwi.

25 ways to spend the time.

Landscapes
  • The Ngorongoro CraterA collapsed caldera twenty kilometres across with its own permanent population of thirty thousand animals.
  • Kilimanjaro, 5,895 mThe highest free-standing mountain on earth: rainforest, moorland, alpine desert and glacier in five days of walking.
  • Materuni, Under KilimanjaroEighty metres of cold water through Chagga coffee farms on the lower slopes, an easy walk from Moshi.
  • Manyara and the RufijiFlamingo and tree-climbing lions at Manyara; the Rufiji braiding through Nyerere, the largest park in Africa.
  • Zanzibar, Nungwi to PajeWhite sand, warm shallow water and dhows still built by hand on the beach at Nungwi.
  • Udzungwa and JozaniThe Udzungwa scarp holds species found nowhere else; Jozani holds the red colobus, also found nowhere else.
Wildlife
  • The Big Five, and Then SomeLion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhino, plus the largest elephant herds left in East Africa.
  • Following the MigrationCalving on the southern plains in February, the Grumeti in June, the Mara river in August — the circuit never stops.
  • Ngorongoro, Shared GroundThe conservation area is the rare model where Maasai pastoralists and wildlife legally use the same land.
  • Snorkelling MnembaThe atoll off the northeast tip of Zanzibar, plus kite surfing at Paje and diving at Pemba.
People and culture
  • Maasai, Chagga, SwahiliCattle-keepers on the northern plains, coffee farmers on the mountain, and a coast that has always faced east.
  • The Hadzabe of Lake EyasiOne of the last hunter-gatherer peoples in Africa, speaking a click language unrelated to anything nearby.
  • Sauti za BusaraFour days of live music from across the continent inside the old Arab fort in Stone Town, every February.
  • Tingatinga and MakondeEnamel-bright Tingatinga painting from Dar es Salaam, and Makonde ebony carving from the southern plateau.
  • Spice, Ugali, UrojoCloves and cardamom from the Zanzibar plantations, grilled seafood at Forodhani, and ugali everywhere else.
  • Boma and Coffee FarmMaasai boma visits on the crater rim road, and Chagga coffee walks on the lower slopes of Kilimanjaro.
  • Dala Dalas and DhowsMinibuses inland, dhows on the water, and a fish auction at Kivukoni that starts before it is light.
Places
  • Dar es SalaamHarbour of peace: the ferry terminal, the fish market at Kivukoni, and the dhows still working the channel.
  • The Doors of Stone TownBrass-studded Indian doors and carved Omani frames, on coral-rag houses in alleys too narrow to drive.
  • Stone Town and KilwaStone Town is UNESCO-listed; Kilwa Kisiwani was the richest port on the Swahili coast six centuries ago.
  • Olduvai and the CoastOlduvai Gorge holds the earliest human record; the coast holds a thousand years of Indian Ocean trade.
Ways to travel
  • Uhuru Peak by MachameSix days up the Machame route, one very long night to the summit, and a sunrise you have to earn.
  • Camps and Spice IslandsMobile camps that follow the migration, and beach houses on the Zanzibar east coast with nothing either side.
  • Acacia, Kopje, SunsetThe Serengeti kopjes at the end of the day, and the migration column stretching past the edge of the frame.
  • Ruaha and KataviThe south and west: huge elephant populations, hippo crowded into shrinking pools, and no other vehicles.
