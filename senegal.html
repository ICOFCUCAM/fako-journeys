01Highlights

What Senegal is known for.

Six of the twenty-seven, and the ones most people come for. The rest are below — and none of them is filler.

Nature & Landscapes

Delta and Dune

The Sine-Saloum mangrove delta, the Casamance forest, and Sahel sand in the north.

Wildlife

Djoudj

Three million migrating birds winter in the Djoudj — one of the great bird sites on earth.

Mountains

Fouta Djallon Foothills

No high peaks — the ground rises to the Fouta Djallon foothills in the far south-east.

Beaches & Coast

The Petite Côte

Saly, Somone and Cap Skirring — Atlantic sand from the peninsula down to Casamance.

Forests & Rainforest

Casamance

Palm and rice country south of the Gambia, greener than the rest and quite different.

Culture

Teranga

The word means hospitality, and it is the thing the country most wants you to notice.

25 ways to spend the time.

Landscapes
  • Delta and DuneThe Sine-Saloum mangrove delta, the Casamance forest, and Sahel sand in the north.
  • Fouta Djallon FoothillsNo high peaks — the ground rises to the Fouta Djallon foothills in the far south-east.
  • DindefeloA hundred metres of water off the Fouta escarpment, near the Guinea border.
  • The Senegal and the SaloumThe Senegal river along the Mauritanian border, and the Saloum spreading into the sea.
  • The Petite CôteSaly, Somone and Cap Skirring — Atlantic sand from the peninsula down to Casamance.
  • CasamancePalm and rice country south of the Gambia, greener than the rest and quite different.
Wildlife
  • DjoudjThree million migrating birds winter in the Djoudj — one of the great bird sites on earth.
  • Niokolo-KobaLion, hippo and Derby eland in the south-east, in a park that is slowly recovering.
  • Community CampementsThe Casamance campements villageois — village-owned, village-run, and thirty years old.
  • Surf and SailDakar's breaks, kite-surfing on the Petite Côte, and pirogue trips through the mangrove.
People and culture
  • TerangaThe word means hospitality, and it is the thing the country most wants you to notice.
  • Wolof, Serer and DiolaWolof across the country, Serer in the delta, and the Diola of Casamance.
  • Dak'Art and the MagalThe Dakar art biennale, and the Grand Magal of Touba, which moves millions of people.
  • Glass Painting and WeavingSous-verre glass painting, and the strip-woven cloth of the Manjak weavers.
  • ThieboudienneRice and fish, cooked in one pot, and the national dish by common consent.
  • Short Flights, Long BeachesSix hours from Europe, and beaches with a shallow warm Atlantic in front of them.
  • Ataya and the MarketThree glasses of tea, poured from height, and each one stronger than the last.
Places
  • DakarThe most westerly city in Africa, on a peninsula, with the ocean on three sides.
  • Colonial and Sudano-SahelianThe balconies of Saint-Louis, and the mud-and-timber mosques of the interior.
  • GoréeThe island off Dakar, and the house of slaves that stands on it.
  • Saint-LouisThe old colonial capital on its island in the river mouth, with the Faidherbe bridge.
Ways to travel
  • Surf, Sail, PaddleDakar's surf breaks, pirogues in the delta, and the Paris-Dakar's original finish line.
  • Delta LodgesSmall lodges on the Saloum islands, reached only by boat, with birds outside.
  • Light on WaterDelta light at either end of the day, and pink water at the Lac Rose.
  • Bassari CountryThe Bassari and Bedik villages of the south-east, up in the hills, and rarely visited.
