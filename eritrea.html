Eritrea

Art deco Asmara, and the Dahlak islands.

A modernist capital on a highland plateau that UNESCO listed whole, a steam railway down to the Red Sea, and three hundred islands off Massawa that almost nobody visits.

Landscapes
  • The EscarpmentThe plateau drops two thousand metres to the Red Sea in about ninety kilometres.
  • Emba Soira3,018 metres in the southern highlands, with terraced farming right up the sides.
  • Seasonal OnlyThe highland streams run in the summer rains and are dry the rest of the year.
  • The Red Sea CoastNo permanent rivers to speak of — the water here is the sea, and it is very warm.
  • The Dahlak ArchipelagoThree hundred and fifty islands, four of them inhabited, and reefs almost nobody has dived.
  • The Green BeltSemenawi Bahri, a strip of cloud forest on the escarpment an hour from the capital.
Wildlife
  • Danakil and DahlakWild ass in the Danakil, dugong and turtle in the Dahlak archipelago.
  • Not the Business HereThere is no safari circuit. The wildlife is coastal and marine, and it is worth the swap.
  • Terraces That Hold the SoilHighland terracing built by hand over centuries, and still the reason the topsoil is there.
  • CyclingEritrea is a cycling country — the Tour of Eritrea climbs the escarpment.
People and culture
  • Nine Groups, Two FaithsTigrinya, Tigre, Afar, Bilen and five more, roughly half Christian and half Muslim.
  • The Afar and the RashaidaThe Afar on the southern coast, and the Rashaida who came across from Arabia in the 1840s.
  • Meskel and EidMeskel bonfires in September, and Eid in Massawa, in the same country and often the same street.
  • Filigree and BasketrySilver filigree from Asmara's goldsmiths, and coiled straw baskets from the highlands.
  • Injera, Zigni and MacchiatoEritrean stews on injera, and an Italian coffee culture that never left.
  • The Safest Capital AroundAsmara is small, walkable and calm, and children are everywhere in it after dark.
  • The PasseggiataAsmara walks Harnet Avenue in the evening. Everybody does, and nobody hurries.
Places
  • Asmara and MassawaOne a cool art-deco plateau town, the other a coral-block Ottoman port on the sea.
  • Futurist and CoralFiat Tagliero's concrete wings in Asmara, and Massawa's buildings cut from the reef itself.
  • Adulis and QohaitoA Red Sea port that traded with Rome, and a highland city on the escarpment above it.
  • Asmara, Listed WholeThe first modernist city anywhere to be inscribed as a World Heritage site, in 2017.
Ways to travel
  • The Massawa RoadDown the escarpment by road or by the restored steam railway, two thousand metres in a morning.
  • Modest, and ImprovingA handful of restored Italian-era hotels in Asmara. Nothing on the coast yet.
  • Deco in Low LightAsmara's facades in the late afternoon, when the plaster goes warm and the streets empty.
  • The Tank GraveyardA hillside outside Asmara stacked with the wreckage of thirty years of war.
