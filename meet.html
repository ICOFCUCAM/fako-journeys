The human layer

Africa is not a backdrop.

Every country here was written up through the same twenty-seven headings, and twelve of them are about people. So the same question can be put to all 54 at once — and it comes back 54 different ways, which is the part a photograph of a sunset cannot tell you. 7 questions, 378 answers, none of them about “African culture” in general.

Pick a country to put all seven to one place.

Who lives here?

Africa is not one people. It is roughly two thousand languages, and the answer changes at every border and often inside one.

BeninWest Africa

Fon, Yoruba, Bariba, Somba

The Fon of Dahomey in the south, the Somba in the Atakora with their fortified houses.

Burkina FasoWest Africa

Mossi, Bobo, Lobi, Fulani

The Mossi kingdoms in the centre, the Bobo and Lobi in the west, the Fulani across the north.

CameroonCentral & West Africa

Fulani, Mafa and Bamileke

Two hundred and fifty ethnic groups and as many languages, from the Mafa terraces of the Mandara to the Fulani of the Sahel.

BaAka and Sangha-Sangha

The BaAka in the forest, and the Sangha-Sangha along the river.

ChadCentral & West Africa

The Toubou

A desert people of the Tibesti and Ennedi, and among the most self-sufficient anywhere.

CongoCentral & West Africa

Bantu peoples along the river, and Aka forest hunter-gatherers in the north.

Baoulé weaving, Senufo sculpture, and Dan masks from the western hills.

DR CongoCentral & West Africa

Forest hunter-gatherers of the Ituri, with a relationship to that forest going back millennia.

The Bubi

Bioko's first people, with their own language, and a history separate from the mainland.

Fang across the north, Punu in the south, and Baka forest people in the interior.

Dogon on the escarpment, Bambara on the river, Tuareg and Fulani on the plains north of it.

A coastal people who fish with lanchas and, by tradition, with the help of dolphins.

NigerWest Africa

The Gerewol, where the men dress and dance and the women choose.

Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Fulani

Four large cultures and hundreds of smaller ones, each with its own courts and calendars.

Mende in the south, Temne in the north, and the Krio of the peninsula.

TogoWest Africa

The Batammariba

Their name means 'those who shape the earth', and the houses are the argument.

BurundiEast Africa

One language, Kirundi, spoken by everybody, and a history the country is still working through.

DjiboutiEast Africa

The Afar on the southern coast, and the Rashaida who came across from Arabia in the 1840s.

EthiopiaEast Africa

The lower Omo holds sixteen peoples in one valley, and the visits should be arranged carefully.

KenyaEast Africa

Samburu in the northern drylands, Maasai in the south, both still moving cattle between seasonal grazing.

RwandaEast Africa

Cultural villages near Musanze where former poachers now guide, and the beekeepers and potters of the Virunga foothills.

South SudanEast Africa

The Mundari

One of the last hunter-gatherer peoples in Africa, speaking a click language unrelated to anything nearby.

Tracking with San guides, who will find in an hour what nobody else sees at all.

EswatiniSouthern Africa

One nation, one language, and a set of ceremonies that everyone in the country takes part in.

One people, one language, and a kingdom that kept its independence by going uphill.

MozambiqueSouthern Africa

NamibiaSouthern Africa

The Himba of the Kunene, whose way of life is genuinely intact and should be visited carefully.

South AfricaSouthern Africa

Ndebele wall painting, Zulu beadwork with its own grammar, and Xhosa initiation still practised in the Eastern Cape.

ZambiaSouthern Africa

ZimbabweSouthern Africa

The Tuareg in the southwest around Ghat, the Tebu in the Tibesti foothills in the southeast.

MoroccoNorth Africa

Village life at two thousand metres, where the year is still organised around the snow.

SudanNorth Africa

The Nubians

The Nubian villages along the northern Nile paint their houses, and have done for a very long time.

TunisiaNorth Africa

The ksour of Tataouine, and the underground houses of Matmata.

The Santiago interior and the northern islands, with different histories and different music.

SeychellesIslands
