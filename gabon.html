What Gabon is known for.

Six of the twenty-seven, and the ones most people come for. The rest are below — and none of them is filler.

Nature & Landscapes

Eighty-Eight Per Cent Forest

One of the most forested countries on earth, and eleven per cent of it in national parks.

Wildlife

Lowland Gorillas and Mandrills

Habituated gorillas at Loango, and mandrill hordes at Lope that run to hundreds.

Mountains

The Chaillu Massif

1,575 metres at Mont Iboundji, forested to the top, in the middle of the country.

Beaches & Coast

Eight Hundred Kilometres of Atlantic

Empty beaches with forest behind them, humpbacks offshore, and leatherbacks nesting.

Forests & Rainforest

Congo Basin Forest

Lope has one of the longest continuous records of forest-savanna change anywhere.

Culture

Bwiti

An initiatory religion built around iboga, and the ceremonies run all night to harp and drum.

Landscapes
  • Eighty-Eight Per Cent ForestOne of the most forested countries on earth, and eleven per cent of it in national parks.
  • The Chaillu Massif1,575 metres at Mont Iboundji, forested to the top, in the middle of the country.
  • Kongou and MingouliKongou on the Ivindo — three kilometres wide in flood, in the middle of rainforest.
  • The Ogooue and the IvindoThe Ogooue drains almost the whole country; the Ivindo runs through Ivindo National Park.
  • Eight Hundred Kilometres of AtlanticEmpty beaches with forest behind them, humpbacks offshore, and leatherbacks nesting.
  • Congo Basin ForestLope has one of the longest continuous records of forest-savanna change anywhere.
Wildlife
  • Lowland Gorillas and MandrillsHabituated gorillas at Loango, and mandrill hordes at Lope that run to hundreds.
  • Forest and Savanna at LopeWhere the forest meets the last of the Ogooue savanna, with elephant and buffalo in both.
  • Thirteen Parks in One DecisionGabon created all thirteen national parks in 2002, on one day, covering eleven per cent of the country.
  • Kayak, Trek and BoatLagoon kayaking at Loango, forest trekking at Ivindo, and the train through Lope.
People and culture
  • BwitiAn initiatory religion built around iboga, and the ceremonies run all night to harp and drum.
  • Fang, Punu, Myene and the BakaFang across the north, Punu in the south, and Baka forest people in the interior.
  • The Bwiti NightNgombi harp, mouth bow and dancing until dawn, and it is a religion rather than a show.
  • Masks and Ngombi HarpsPunu white-faced masks, Fang reliquary figures, and the carved harps of Bwiti.
  • Nyembwe and Smoked FishChicken in palm nut nyembwe sauce, and river fish smoked over a slow fire.
  • Elephants From a BoatLoango's lagoon puts you at eye level with elephant and buffalo, safely, from the water.
  • Grilled Fish on the SandThe Libreville beach restaurants at the weekend, and the fish landed that morning.
Places
  • Libreville and Port-GentilAn Atlantic capital, and an oil town on an island at the Ogooue delta.
  • Colonial and ContemporaryFrench colonial along the coast, and Libreville's newer buildings behind it.
  • Lope's Rock ArtEngravings on the Ogooue at Lope, and Iron Age sites going back two and a half thousand years.
  • Lope-Okanda, and IvindoTwo World Heritage sites: the forest-savanna mosaic, and the falls on the Ivindo.
Ways to travel
  • Whales, Turtles and SurfHumpbacks from July, leatherbacks from November, and a beach nobody else is on.
  • A Handful, and GoodLoango and Lope have real lodges, and the rest of the parks are camping.
  • Forest Meeting OceanLoango at low sun, when the elephants come out and the beach is empty in both directions.
  • The Trans-GabonaisThe railway from Libreville to Franceville, straight through Lope, overnight.
