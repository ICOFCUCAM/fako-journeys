Mali

Timbuktu, Djenne, and the Niger bend.

The mud-brick mosque at Djenne is the largest building of its kind on earth, the Bandiagara escarpment holds Dogon villages built into the cliff, and the Niger loops nine hundred kilometres north into the desert before turning back.

Landscapes
  • The Niger BendThe river runs north into the Sahara, thinks better of it, and turns southeast.
  • The Bandiagara EscarpmentA hundred and fifty kilometres of sandstone cliff, with villages built into the face of it.
  • Falls at WoroniSmall falls on the sandstone plateau above Bandiagara, running only after the rains.
  • The Niger and Lake DeboPinasses working the river between Mopti and Timbuktu, and the inland delta spreading behind them.
  • River Sand, Not SeaLandlocked — the beaches here are sandbars on the Niger, and people swim off them.
  • The Gallery ForestRibbons of woodland along the river, and the baobabs standing alone on the plain behind.
Wildlife
  • Elephants in the GourmaThe Gourma herd makes the longest elephant migration in Africa, on a loop into Burkina Faso.
  • Not the Business HereThere is no lodge circuit. The Gourma elephants are a conservation story, not a game drive.
  • Re-Plastering DjenneEvery year the town rebuilds the mosque's surface by hand in a single day.
  • The Escarpment on FootThe Dogon country is walking country, and always has been.
People and culture
  • Where the Blues Came FromAli Farka Toure, Toumani Diabate, Salif Keita and Tinariwen are all from here.
  • Dogon, Bambara, Tuareg, FulaniDogon on the escarpment, Bambara on the river, Tuareg and Fulani on the plains north of it.
  • The Sigui and the DamaDogon masked ceremonies, the Sigui every sixty years and the Dama for the dead.
  • Bogolan and Bamana SculptureMud-dyed bogolanfini cloth, Bamana chiwara antelope crests, and Dogon carved doors.
  • Tigadege Na and CapitaineGroundnut stew, and Nile perch grilled whole on the riverbank at Mopti.
  • Hard Country for ItThe security situation makes most of Mali unvisitable at present, and that is the honest answer.
  • Monday Market at DjenneThe whole delta comes to the square in front of the mosque, once a week.
Places
  • Bamako and MoptiA capital sprawling both sides of the Niger, and a port town built on three islands.
  • Sudano-SahelianMud walls, projecting palm beams, and buttressed towers — a style that belongs to this river.
  • TimbuktuThree mud mosques, and a library tradition that ran to hundreds of thousands of manuscripts.
  • Four Sites, All at RiskDjenne, Timbuktu, Bandiagara and the Tomb of Askia — every one of them on the danger list.
Ways to travel
  • Walking the Dogon CountryVillage to village along the escarpment, sleeping on roofs, four days or fourteen.
  • Almost NoneA handful of hotels in Bamako. The rest of the country is guesthouses and roofs.
  • Mud and Long ShadowsDjenne an hour before sunset, when the toron beams throw stripes down the wall.
  • The Tomb of AskiaA pyramid of mud at Gao, built in 1495 for the emperor of Songhai.
