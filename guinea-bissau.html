Guinea-Bissau

The Bijagos, and eighty-eight islands.

An archipelago of eighty-eight islands with saltwater hippos, nesting turtles and a matrilineal society that still chooses its own kings — and a mainland of mangrove and rice paddy behind it.

Landscapes
  • Mangrove and TideThe tidal range here is five metres, and the channels between the islands change with it.
  • Flat CountryThe highest point is three hundred metres. This is a country of water, not relief.
  • None to Speak OfNo falls — the land has nowhere to fall from. The water here moves sideways with the tide.
  • The Geba and the CorubalTwo rivers cutting deep estuaries into the mainland, navigable a long way up.
  • Island SandBubaque, Rubane and Orango have empty palm-backed beaches and warm water.
  • Palm and Sacred GroveOil palm through the islands, and groves that are ceremonial ground and off limits.
Wildlife
  • Saltwater HipposThe hippos of Orango swim in the sea between islands, which almost no others do.
  • Turtles at PoilaoOne of the largest green turtle nesting sites in the Atlantic, on an uninhabited island.
  • Community ConservationThe island communities police the turtle beaches themselves, and it works.
  • Fishing the ChannelsBarracuda and tarpon in the passes between islands, on light tackle.
People and culture
  • Matrilineal, and Still SoBijago women choose husbands, own the houses, and hold the religious authority.
  • The BijagosAge grades govern life, and the vaca-bruto initiation is danced in a bull mask.
  • Carnival in BissauMasks, drums and neighbourhood competition, Portuguese in form and entirely local in content.
  • Carving and ClothBijago bull and shark masks, and the woven pano de pente cloth used as currency.
  • Rice, Cashew and BicaRice from the paddies, cashew as the whole export economy, and Portuguese coffee.
  • Boats and BeachesNot for very small children — the boats are open and the tides are serious.
  • Sunday at the BeachBissau goes to Bubaque and the near islands at weekends, by ferry, with a stereo.
Places
  • BissauA small Portuguese-built capital with a decaying Bissau Velho quarter and very good music.
  • Portuguese Colonial and Palm ThatchTiled colonial fronts in Bissau, and rectangular palm-thatch houses through the islands.
  • CacheuA slaving fort of 1588 on the river, and one of the oldest European buildings in West Africa.
  • The Bijagos BiosphereThe whole archipelago is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, run with the island communities.
Ways to travel
  • Island Hopping by PirogueThere are no bridges. Everything is boats, tides and waiting, and that is the pleasure of it.
  • Two or Three LodgesSmall fishing and eco-lodges on Rubane and Orango, and not much else anywhere.
  • Tide and PalmThe channels at low water, when the mangrove roots come out and the light goes flat.
  • OrangoHippos, an island king, and a lodge with no road on the island at all.
