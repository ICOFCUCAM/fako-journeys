What Mauritania is known for.

Six of the twenty-seven, and the ones most people come for. The rest are below — and none of them is filler.

Nature & Landscapes

The Adrar

A sandstone plateau cut by palm-filled canyons, with the old caravan towns in them.

Wildlife

The Banc d'Arguin

Two million wading birds winter here, on the largest tidal flats in West Africa.

Mountains

The Kediet ej Jill

915 metres of magnetite near Zouerat, so iron-rich it deflects a compass.

Beaches & Coast

Seven Hundred Kilometres of Atlantic

Cold current, big surf, and the Imraguen fishing villages working with dolphins.

Forests & Rainforest

The Palmeraies

Date palm groves in the Adrar canyons — the only shade for hundreds of kilometres.

Culture

Moor and Fulani

Bidhan and Haratin Moors across the north, Fulani, Soninke and Wolof along the river.

Landscapes
  • The AdrarA sandstone plateau cut by palm-filled canyons, with the old caravan towns in them.
  • The Kediet ej Jill915 metres of magnetite near Zouerat, so iron-rich it deflects a compass.
  • Guelta de MatmataPermanent pools in the rock south of Atar, with crocodiles that have been there since the Sahara was wet.
  • The Senegal RiverThe southern border, and the only permanent river in the country.
  • Seven Hundred Kilometres of AtlanticCold current, big surf, and the Imraguen fishing villages working with dolphins.
  • The PalmeraiesDate palm groves in the Adrar canyons — the only shade for hundreds of kilometres.
Wildlife
  • The Banc d'ArguinTwo million wading birds winter here, on the largest tidal flats in West Africa.
  • Desert, Not SavannaThe wildlife is birds and desert-adapted mammals, and the coast is where it concentrates.
  • Two Million BirdsThe Banc d'Arguin is a Ramsar site and one of the most important wintering grounds on earth.
  • Trekking the AdrarCamel treks between the ksour, days at a time, sleeping out on the sand.
People and culture
  • Moor and FulaniBidhan and Haratin Moors across the north, Fulani, Soninke and Wolof along the river.
  • The ImraguenA coastal people who fish with lanchas and, by tradition, with the help of dolphins.
  • The Chinguetti FestivalPoetry, camel racing and manuscript readings in the old caravan town.
  • Silver, Leather and ChestsMoorish silver, tooled leather cushions, and the painted wooden chests of the Adrar.
  • Thieboudienne and Three TeasFish and rice from the river south, and the three-glass tea ceremony everywhere.
  • Big Country for Small ChildrenDistances are long and facilities are few. Better for teenagers than for toddlers.
  • The Fish Market at SunsetThe pirogues come in at Nouakchott beach and the whole catch is sold on the sand.
Places
  • Nouakchott and NouadhibouA capital built from nothing in 1958, and a port with the world's biggest ship graveyard.
  • Dry Stone and SandKsour built of flat stone without mortar, and streets that the dunes are taking back.
  • Chinguetti and OuadaneTwelfth-century caravan towns in dry stone, half-buried by the dunes now.
  • The Manuscript LibrariesFamily libraries in Chinguetti holding astronomy, law and poetry from the middle ages.
Ways to travel
  • Riding the Ore TrainTwenty hours in the open on top of iron ore, in the cold, and people do it on purpose.
  • Small and SeriousAuberges in Atar and Chinguetti run by people who know the desert, and little else.
  • Sand Against StoneChinguetti in the last hour, when the dunes in the streets are lit from the side.
  • TerjitA hot spring under palms in a narrow gorge, and the best shade in the country.
